# Clinical coder

> This is an illustrative reference profile for a generic digital health care organisation. It is not an official job description for any employer, and its job evaluation scores are not a formal evaluation.

**Family:** [Data](../index.md#data)  
**Bands:** 3, 4, 5, 6, 7  
**UK GDaD PCF role:** none (this reference defines the role)  
**ESCO occupations:** [clinical coder](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/6219f0ef-6414-4d01-9221-41ac25dcb409) (ISCO-08 3252)

## Summary

Clinical coders read patients' health records and translate diagnoses, procedures, and other clinical information into standard classification codes, such as ICD for diagnoses and a procedure classification for interventions. Coded data is used for payment, service planning, clinical audit, research, and public health statistics, so its accuracy matters well beyond the coding team. Coders work closely with clinicians to clarify records, and increasingly use clinical terminologies such as SNOMED CT and computer-assisted coding tools.

## In a digital health care organisation

- Coders read complete case notes, which often describe serious illness, injury, abuse, self-harm, and death, so the work is emotionally demanding and teams need good support and supervision.
- Coded data drives payment for activity, mortality and outcome indicators, and service planning, so errors can affect funding, published performance, and decisions about care.
- Coding depends on the quality of clinical documentation, so coders query unclear records with clinicians and help improve how care is recorded.
- Coders handle full identifiable health records every day, so confidentiality and correct access are essential.
- Automated and computer-assisted coding, and mapping from SNOMED CT to classifications, are changing the work, and coders check that automated codes are correct.

## Band 3: Trainee clinical coder

A trainee clinical coder learns to code straightforward hospital episodes under close supervision, working towards a clinical coding qualification.

### Responsibilities

- Code straightforward episodes, such as day cases, from health records using classification rules, with all work checked by a senior coder.
- Learn medical terms, anatomy, and the national and international coding standards through structured training.
- Find, track, and return health records correctly, and keep them secure.
- Record coded data in the patient administration or coding system accurately and on time.
- Raise unclear or missing information with a senior coder.

## Band 4: Clinical coder

A clinical coder codes a full range of routine episodes independently and accurately, meeting agreed deadlines and quality standards.

### Responsibilities

- Code inpatient, day case, and outpatient episodes accurately across common specialties, using classification rules and local policies.
- Query unclear, missing, or conflicting clinical information with clinicians and record the outcome.
- Check codes suggested by computer-assisted coding or terminology maps, and correct them where needed.
- Meet deadlines for coding completeness, so data is ready for payment and reporting.
- Take part in coding audits and act on their findings.

## Band 5: Senior clinical coder

A senior clinical coder codes complex episodes across specialties, acts as a source of expert advice for other coders, and checks and trains less experienced coders.

### Responsibilities

- Code complex episodes, such as critical care, cancer, multiple procedures, and deaths, across specialties.
- Resolve difficult coding questions with consultants and other senior clinicians, explaining coding rules clearly.
- Check the work of trainee coders and give them feedback and on-the-job training.
- Validate coded data before it is submitted and fix errors with the right team.
- Work with clinical teams to improve the quality of clinical documentation.

## Band 6: Clinical coding auditor and trainer

A clinical coding auditor and trainer audits the accuracy of coded data, designs and delivers coding training, and advises the organisation on coding standards. In some organisations this level also leads a coding team day to day.

### Responsibilities

- Plan and carry out coding audits across specialties, measuring accuracy against national and international standards.
- Report audit findings to coding, clinical, finance, and information teams, and agree improvement actions.
- Design and deliver coding training, from foundation courses for new coders to refresher training on standard changes.
- Advise clinicians and managers on how coding affects payment, mortality indicators, and performance data.
- Lead the introduction of changes in classification versions, coding standards, and coding tools.
- Lead a coding team day to day where the organisation combines this with audit and training.

## Band 7: Clinical coding manager

A clinical coding manager leads the organisation's clinical coding service, including its coding teams, quality programme, and links with clinical, finance, and information teams.

### Responsibilities

- Lead the clinical coding service and its team leaders, making sure coding is complete, accurate, and on time.
- Set coding policies and the coding quality programme, in line with national and international standards.
- Hold the coding budget and plan the workforce, including recruitment, training, and accreditation.
- Advise senior leaders on how coding affects funding, mortality and outcome indicators, and published data.
- Lead the adoption of new coding technology, such as computer-assisted coding and terminology mapping, with clinical safety and information governance colleagues.
- Support the wellbeing of coders who regularly read distressing records.
