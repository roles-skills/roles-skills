## Summary

Test managers plan, lead, and assure testing for the organisation's digital health services and programmes. They decide how much testing is needed and of what kind, manage test teams, environments, and suppliers, and tell decision makers clearly what risks remain before a service goes live. In health care this includes making sure testing supports the clinical safety case and any regulatory requirements.

## In a digital health care organisation

- Go-live decisions for clinical systems depend on test evidence and residual risks, which test managers report to product owners, clinical safety officers, and programme boards.
- Large changes, such as electronic patient record upgrades, need testing across many departments, systems, and suppliers, and often clinical staff time for acceptance testing.
- Testing must fit around clinical services, such as planning cutover tests and dress rehearsals to keep disruption to patient care low.
- Regulated software and safety-critical changes need testing that meets standards such as IEC 62304 and the organisation's clinical risk management process.

## UK GDaD PCF role description

> A test manager is responsible for planning, monitoring and documenting testing. They implement a test strategy to ensure quality principles are delivered on time and within budget.
> 
> In this role, you will:
> - create and implement test plans and approaches to mitigate risk
> - monitor and control test activities, ensuring compliance with standards
> - communicate risks and assess and report timely test status’ to stakeholders
> - define metrics for monitoring and controlling test activities
> - provide direction on complex testing issues, outlining and influencing delivery of projects and programmes
> - identify opportunities for continuous improvement
> - lead and support teams, providing coaching in good testing practices

## Band 7: Test manager

**UK GDaD PCF level: Test manager**

> A test manager works with a team to create appropriate test approaches and plans, considering environments, data, tools, resources, timelines, requirements and risk. They contribute to test strategies and identify opportunities for continuous improvement.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - lead and support a team in testing activities
> - determine and apply appropriate test types, techniques and standards to mitigate risk early
> - manage, maintain and continuously improve test activities, ensuring compliance with regulatory and organisational standards
> - identify and communicate risks
> - oversee the reporting and recording of outcomes, including defects
> - contribute to and agree testing standards, and enable others to follow them

### Responsibilities

- Lead test planning for a product or programme, covering environments, data, tools, resources, timelines, and clinical risk.
- Plan user acceptance testing with clinical and operational leads, and secure clinicians' time to take part.
- Manage a team of test analysts and engineers, including supplier testers.
- Make sure testing meets clinical safety, information governance, and regulatory requirements.
- Report test progress, defects, and residual risks to product owners, clinical safety officers, and programme boards.
- Improve test processes and agree testing standards with delivery teams.

## Band 8c: Head of test

**UK GDaD PCF level: Head of test**

> A head of test sets the strategy for test quality and influences testing practices across a broad area. They develop, monitor and evaluate testing standards, practices, methods and specifications, and make strategic decisions that improve testing in an organisation.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - lead and influence testing activities to ensure the organisation implements quality test frameworks
> - select and guide the implementation of testing tools and technologies
> - define good practices, standards and ways of working, continuously improving testing capability
> - escalate risks to senior stakeholders
> - contribute to cross-government test communities to improve test practices
> - lead the organisation in adhering to test industry compliance and regulatory standards

### Responsibilities

- Set the organisation's testing strategy, standards, and frameworks for digital health services.
- Make sure the organisation's testing meets clinical safety, medical device, and other regulatory requirements.
- Choose testing tools and technologies, and manage the testing budget and supplier contracts.
- Advise senior leaders on quality and safety risks before major releases and go-live decisions.
- Lead and develop test managers, analysts, and engineers, and build the organisation's testing capability.

## ESCO occupations and skills

### ICT quality assurance manager

- **URI:** <http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/a28dff76-74df-46ee-929f-d1fffbb63cd7>
- **ESCO code:** 2519.5  ·  **ISCO-08:** 2519 Software and applications developers and analysts not elsewhere classified
- **Alternative labels:** ICT quality manager; ICT test manager; IT quality assurance manager; ICT quality assurance managers; principal test manager; ICT principal testing manager

> ICT quality assurance managers establish and operate an ICT quality approach through quality management systems, in compliance with internal and external standards and the organisation's culture. They ensure that the management controls are correctly implemented to safeguard asset, data integrity and operations. They focus on the achievement of quality goals, including the maintenance of the external certification according to quality standards and monitor statistics to forecast quality outcomes.
