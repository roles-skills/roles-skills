# Executive assistant

> This is an illustrative reference profile for a generic digital health care organisation. It is not an official job description for any employer, and its job evaluation scores are not a formal evaluation.

**Family:** [Corporate services](../index.md#corporate-services)  
**Bands:** 4, 5, 6  
**UK GDaD PCF role:** none (this reference defines the role)  
**ESCO occupations:** [executive assistant](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/2a04a1df-a3b9-403e-a245-e7f93fdddd44) (ISCO-08 3343)

## Summary

Executive assistants give senior leaders high-level administrative and business support. They manage diaries, correspondence, meetings, and papers, act as a point of contact for the leader's office, and use judgement to deal with matters on the leader's behalf. They often handle highly confidential and sensitive information.

## In a digital health care organisation

- Senior leaders in health care deal with patient safety incidents, complaints, and staff cases, so executive assistants regularly handle sensitive and confidential material.
- Executive offices work closely with boards and committees, so accurate papers, minutes, and governance records matter.
- Diaries must make room for urgent matters, such as serious incidents and service outages, at short notice.

## Band 4: Executive assistant

Provides executive support to a director or senior leader, managing their diary, correspondence, and meetings.

### Responsibilities

- Manage a senior leader's diary, setting priorities and resolving clashes.
- Handle correspondence, drafting replies and dealing with routine matters on the leader's behalf.
- Prepare papers, presentations, and briefings for meetings.
- Take minutes and track actions for the leader's meetings.
- Act as the first point of contact for the leader's office, using tact and discretion.
- Arrange travel, events, and visits.

- **Leadership:** May supervise a small team or coordinate others' work.
- **Accountability:** Delivery of a defined service or process.

## Band 5: Senior executive assistant

Provides executive support to the chief executive, chair, or an executive director, and coordinates the business of their office.

### Responsibilities

- Manage the diary and priorities of the chief executive or an executive director.
- Handle sensitive correspondence, including complaints and enquiries from partners and regulators.
- Coordinate executive team meetings, papers, and action logs.
- Take formal minutes for executive and committee meetings.
- Track commitments and deadlines across the executive office and chase progress.
- Supervise other assistants and cover across the executive office.

## Band 6: Executive office manager

Manages the executive office, its staff, and its business cycle, and makes sure the executive team and board are well supported.

### Responsibilities

- Manage the executive office team, setting priorities and supporting their development.
- Plan the business cycle for the executive team, including forward plans and papers.
- Make sure briefings, papers, and correspondence are accurate, timely, and well judged.
- Coordinate the executive response to urgent matters, such as serious incidents and enquiries.
- Manage the executive office budget and its systems.
- Improve executive office processes and tools.
