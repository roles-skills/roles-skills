# Communications officer

> This is an illustrative reference profile for a generic digital health care organisation. It is not an official job description for any employer, and its job evaluation scores are not a formal evaluation.

**Family:** [Corporate services](../index.md#corporate-services)  
**Bands:** 4, 5, 6, 7, 8b  
**UK GDaD PCF role:** none (this reference defines the role)  
**ESCO occupations:** [public relations officer](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/c0489e22-6a1b-4e52-974a-eb71bbb42134) (ISCO-08 2432); [communication manager](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/cd7b838c-51f6-41ec-9067-bb974c0f991b) (ISCO-08 1222)

## Summary

Communications staff help the organisation explain what it does to staff, patients, the public, partners, and the media. They write and publish content, run internal communications and campaigns, manage digital and social media channels, handle media enquiries, and protect the organisation's reputation, especially during incidents and outages.

## In a digital health care organisation

- Communications about health services must be accurate, clear, and accessible, because patients may act on them.
- Campaigns often aim to change behaviour, such as encouraging patients and care staff to use a new digital service.
- Service outages, cyber incidents, and data breaches need fast, careful communications with patients, staff, partners, and the media.
- Stories about patients need consent and must protect confidentiality.

## Band 4: Communications officer

Writes and publishes content for internal and external channels and supports the communications team with routine media and campaign work.

### Responsibilities

- Write and edit news, intranet content, newsletters, and social media posts.
- Publish approved content on the website, intranet, and social media.
- Log media enquiries and monitor coverage.
- Support campaigns and events with content and logistics.
- Check content for accuracy, accessibility, and plain English.

- Advise colleagues on writing clearly for patients and the public.

## Band 6: Communications lead

Leads communications for a major programme or area, such as internal communications or digital channels, and handles sensitive media work.

### Responsibilities

- Lead communications and engagement for a major programme, such as a new clinical system.
- Plan and evaluate campaigns that encourage patients and staff to adopt digital services.
- Handle sensitive media enquiries and prepare spokespeople.
- Plan communications for incidents and service outages with operational teams.
- Advise leaders on how to communicate change and difficult messages.
- Supervise communications officers.

## Band 8b: Head of communications

Leads communications and engagement for the organisation, sets its strategy, and advises the board on reputation.

### Responsibilities

- Lead crisis communications during major incidents.
- Build relationships with senior partners, journalists, and patient groups.
- Shape how the organisation explains its digital services and its use of data to the public.

### communication manager

- **URI:** <http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/cd7b838c-51f6-41ec-9067-bb974c0f991b>
- **ESCO code:** 1222.1  ·  **ISCO-08:** 1222 Advertising and public relations managers
- **Alternative labels:** communication practitioner; public relations manager; communications associate; public relations and communication manager; communications manager; social media manager; press spokesperson; internal communications manager; foreign language correspondent communications manager; external communications manager; corporate communication manager; online communication manager

> Communication managers are responsible for developing communication strategies in order to promote the organization's mission, services or product. They coordinate communication projects and manage the communications issued by the company for both the internal and the external clients. They supervise internal communications, ensuring that communications reach each one of the employees and further questions can be answered. For external communications, they coordinate coherence among the messages transmitted in mails, printed materials, press articles, and corporate promotional materials. They strive to maintain truthful communications.
