# Learning and development specialist

> This is an illustrative reference profile for a generic digital health care organisation. It is not an official job description for any employer, and its job evaluation scores are not a formal evaluation.

**Family:** [Human resources](../index.md#human-resources)  
**Bands:** 4, 5, 6, 7, 8a  
**UK GDaD PCF role:** none (this reference defines the role)  
**ESCO occupations:** [corporate trainer](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/0ba06640-e0ac-4911-9e43-289a8e41651e) (ISCO-08 2424); [corporate training manager](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/a14e96a7-6c4d-4d69-8c6f-7ccadf77bac5) (ISCO-08 1212)

## Summary

Learning and development specialists help staff gain the skills they need to deliver safe, modern, digitally enabled care. They analyse learning needs, design and deliver learning in person and online, run the learning management system, and build career pathways. In a digital health care organisation a large part of their work is growing digital, data, and technology skills, both for specialists and for the whole workforce.

## In a digital health care organisation

- Clinical and care staff need training on new clinical systems before go-live, often across shifts and sites, so learning is planned around services that run 24/7.
- Some training is mandatory, such as information governance, cyber security awareness, and safeguarding, and compliance is reported to the board.
- Growing scarce digital skills from within, through apprenticeships, reskilling, and career pathways, is often faster and cheaper than recruiting them.
- Skills frameworks such as this reference, the UK GDaD PCF, and ESCO give a shared language for skills gap analysis and development plans.
- Learning must be accessible to staff with different needs, working patterns, and levels of digital confidence.

## Band 4: Learning and development coordinator

Coordinates the organisation's learning offer, running bookings, the learning management system, and training records, and supports the delivery of courses.

### Responsibilities

- Schedule courses, book rooms and trainers, and manage bookings and waiting lists.
- Maintain courses, enrolments, and completion records in the learning management system.
- Produce reports on mandatory training compliance for managers.
- Answer questions from staff and managers about the learning offer.
- Support trainers in sessions, including setting up online classrooms and collecting feedback.
- Administer apprenticeship enrolments and reviews with training providers.

## Band 5: Learning and development officer

Designs and delivers learning for a range of staff groups, including digital skills and system training, and evaluates its effect.

### Responsibilities

- Deliver training in person and online, such as induction, digital skills, and management essentials.
- Build e-learning and short guides in the learning management system, making them accessible.
- Work with digital teams to plan and deliver training for new clinical and business systems before go-live.
- Support managers to identify team learning needs and plan development.
- Evaluate courses and improve them using feedback and results.
- Coordinate apprenticeships, working with providers, learners, and managers.

## Band 6: Learning and development specialist

Leads the analysis, design, and evaluation of learning programmes for a major area, such as leadership, digital skills, or system training, working with broad freedom within the learning strategy.

### Responsibilities

- Analyse learning needs for a staff group or profession, using skills gap analysis against this reference.
- Design blended learning programmes with clear outcomes, from e-learning to coaching and practice in the workplace.
- Design training strategies for large clinical system changes, including super-user networks.
- Evaluate the effect of learning on skills, performance, and services, and report it.
- Commission and quality-assure external training providers.
- Coach and develop learning and development officers.

## Band 7: Digital skills lead

Leads the organisation's programme to grow digital, data, and technology skills, for specialist professions and for the whole workforce, using skills frameworks to plan careers and close gaps.

### Responsibilities

- Lead the digital skills programme, including digital literacy for all staff and development for digital, data, and technology professions.
- Run skills gap analysis across digital teams using the role profiles and skills in this reference, and report the results.
- Design career pathways from apprenticeships to senior roles, linked to bands and job evaluation.
- Build partnerships with universities, colleges, and training providers, and make full use of apprenticeship funding.
- Manage the digital skills budget and evaluate return on investment.
- Lead a small team of learning and development staff.
