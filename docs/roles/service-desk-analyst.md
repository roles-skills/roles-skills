## Summary

Service desk analysts are the first point of contact for staff who need help with the organisation's digital services, from login and smartcard problems to faults in clinical systems and devices. They log, triage, resolve, or escalate incidents and requests, keep users informed, and spot patterns that point to wider problems. In many health care organisations the service desk runs around the clock, because clinical services do.

## In a digital health care organisation

- Callers are often clinicians and care staff in the middle of patient care, so the desk prioritises by the effect on patients, not only by order of arrival.
- Many clinical services run 24 hours a day, every day, so the service desk often works shifts and out-of-hours rotas.
- Access to clinical systems uses role-based access and strong authentication, so the desk handles account, smartcard, and multi-factor authentication problems while checking identity carefully.
- Callers may share confidential patient information while describing a fault, so analysts follow information governance rules for what they record in tickets.
- The desk is often the first to see signs of a major incident or a cyber attack, such as many calls about the same system or a wave of suspicious emails, and must escalate them quickly.

## Band 3: Service desk analyst

A service desk analyst answers calls and tickets from staff, resolves common issues at first contact using scripts and knowledge articles, and passes other issues to the right team.

**UK GDaD PCF level: Service desk analyst**

> A service desk analyst interacts with customers.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - perform tasks such as taking calls from customers and trying to resolve their queries
> - know when to escalate issues to your manager

### Responsibilities

- Answer calls, chats, and self-service tickets from staff, and log each one accurately in the service management tool.
- Resolve common issues at first contact using scripts and knowledge articles, such as password resets, account unlocks, and printer faults.
- Check the caller's identity before changing accounts or access, following the organisation's procedures.
- Ask how the issue affects patient care, set the priority, and pass the incident to the right resolver team.
- Keep users updated on their tickets and confirm that the fix works before closing them.
- Record only the information needed in tickets, without unnecessary patient details.

## Band 4: Technical service desk analyst

A technical service desk analyst resolves more complex faults at first contact, including remote fixes to devices and access to clinical systems, and handles urgent calls from clinical areas.

**UK GDaD PCF level: Service desk analyst**

> A service desk analyst interacts with customers.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - perform tasks such as taking calls from customers and trying to resolve their queries
> - know when to escalate issues to your manager

### Responsibilities

- Resolve more complex incidents and requests remotely, including device, software, and clinical system access faults.
- Handle urgent calls from clinical areas, such as emergency departments, wards, and theatres, and start the major incident process when needed.
- Process access requests for clinical systems, checking them against role-based access rules.
- Write and improve knowledge articles so that more issues can be resolved at first contact.
- Spot repeated calls about the same fault and raise them with incident and problem management.
- Report suspected phishing emails and other security events to the security team.
- Show new analysts how to use the desk's tools and procedures.

## Band 5: Senior service desk analyst

**UK GDaD PCF level: Senior service desk analyst**

> A senior service desk analyst monitors inbound calls, supports operators in service resolution and intervenes in difficult calls.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - own and update the script for service desk analysts
> - coach and develop one or more service desk analysts, and manage availability

### Responsibilities

- Lead a shift on the service desk, balancing call queues against the clinical urgency of open incidents.
- Take over difficult or escalated calls, including calls from senior clinicians during urgent care.
- Keep scripts and knowledge articles accurate as clinical systems and devices change.
- Act as the out-of-hours escalation point for the desk, and start the major incident process when many users are affected.
- Check the quality of tickets, including identity checks and what is recorded about patients.
- Plan rota cover so the desk is staffed at all times.

## Band 6: Service desk manager

**UK GDaD PCF level: Service desk manager**

> A service desk manager identifies emerging issues and is a knowledge expert.
> 
> They are responsible for the resourcing and development of the team, including the preparation and co-ordination of service transition activities.

### Responsibilities

- Run the service desk day to day, making sure it is staffed and responsive 24 hours a day, every day.
- Set priorities and response targets that reflect clinical risk, agreed with clinical and operational leads.
- Prepare the desk for new and changed clinical systems, including training, scripts, and extra cover at go-live.
- Analyse ticket trends to find emerging issues, and work with problem management to remove their causes.
- Make sure the desk follows identity checking, information governance, and security procedures, and acts on audit findings.
- Line manage senior analysts and analysts, including recruitment, performance, and wellbeing for staff working shifts.

## Band 7: Head of service desk

**UK GDaD PCF level: Head of service desk**

> A head of service desk is responsible for managing a team who provides first- and second-line technical support.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - ensure support of existing and emerging information and communications technology (ICT) services, and provide technical advice to project teams
> - ensure that the support team undertakes development so that they can advise on future projects and current issues
> - manage and monitor service desk service level agreements (SLAs) and performance

### Responsibilities

- Set the direction for the service desk, including contact channels, self-service, and support for staff working across sites and in the community.
- Agree service levels with clinical and corporate services, and report performance to senior leaders.
- Make sure the desk can keep working during major incidents, cyber attacks, and system downtime, with clear business continuity procedures.
- Advise project teams on how new clinical and corporate systems will be supported before they go live.
- Manage the service desk budget, tools, and any outsourced or out-of-hours support contracts.
- Lead and develop service desk managers, and build a supportive culture for staff working shifts.

- **Alternative labels:** ICT help desk managers; IT helpdesk manager; ICT service desk manager; software support manager; ICT helpdesk manager; IT help desk manager; ICT helpdesk managers; technical support manager

> ICT help desk managers monitor the delivery of technical support services to clients according to the predefined deadlines.
