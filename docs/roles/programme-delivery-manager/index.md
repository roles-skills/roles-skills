## Summary

Programme delivery managers are accountable for delivering complex digital health services that need several teams, suppliers, or organisations to work together, or that carry high technical, clinical, or public risk. They manage dependencies, budgets, commercial arrangements, and people across the programme, and make sure it delivers its intended benefits for patients, clinicians, and care staff.

## In a digital health care organisation

- Programmes often change how care is delivered across several organisations, so programme delivery managers coordinate clinical, operational, and digital leaders who have different priorities and governance.
- Clinical risk must be managed across the whole programme, not only within each team, so they work with clinical safety officers to keep a programme-level view of hazards and safety cases.
- Programmes frequently involve large contracts with suppliers of clinical systems, so commercial and contract management is a major part of the role.
- Benefits in care, such as shorter waits, safer prescribing, or staff time released, must be defined, owned by the service, and tracked long after the programme closes.

## Band 8b: Programme delivery manager

**UK GDaD PCF level: Programme delivery manager**

> A programme delivery manager is accountable for the delivery of complex products and services that are delivered by multiple teams or have high technical or political risk.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - manage dependencies of varying complexity, potentially planning and feeding into larger programmes and portfolios
> - remove blockers and manage risks, commercials, budgets and people
> - balance objectives and redeploy people and resources as priorities change
> - have an in-depth knowledge of Agile and other methodologies
> - be responsible for understanding, managing and communicating to complex stakeholder groups, balancing priorities
> - be the initial escalation point for the programme
> - have an awareness of the bigger picture
> - support the programme director by overseeing the delivery of their vision for the programme
> - support and coach delivery managers

### Responsibilities

- Lead the delivery of a programme of several teams, such as introducing a new digital service across a care pathway.
- Plan and manage dependencies between teams, suppliers, and partner health and care organisations.
- Keep a programme-level view of clinical risk with the clinical safety officer, and make sure each release has the assurance it needs.
- Manage the programme budget, contracts, and supplier performance.
- Report progress, risks, and benefits to the programme board and senior responsible owner.
- Line manage delivery managers and build a healthy delivery culture across the programme.

## Band 8c: Senior programme delivery manager

**UK GDaD PCF level: Programme delivery manager**

> A programme delivery manager is accountable for the delivery of complex products and services that are delivered by multiple teams or have high technical or political risk.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - manage dependencies of varying complexity, potentially planning and feeding into larger programmes and portfolios
> - remove blockers and manage risks, commercials, budgets and people
> - balance objectives and redeploy people and resources as priorities change
> - have an in-depth knowledge of Agile and other methodologies
> - be responsible for understanding, managing and communicating to complex stakeholder groups, balancing priorities
> - be the initial escalation point for the programme
> - have an awareness of the bigger picture
> - support the programme director by overseeing the delivery of their vision for the programme
> - support and coach delivery managers

### Responsibilities

- Lead the delivery of the organisation's largest or highest-risk programmes, such as replacing the core electronic patient record or a system-wide shared care record.
- Coordinate delivery across several organisations in a care system, aligning their plans, governance, and go-live decisions.
- Lead commercial strategy and negotiation with major suppliers, and manage programme budgets across several years.
- Make sure clinical safety, information governance, and business continuity are assured at programme level before major go-lives.
- Lead and develop programme and delivery managers, and set delivery standards across the portfolio.
- Advise executive leaders on programme risk, options, and recovery.
