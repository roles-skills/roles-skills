**ESCO occupations:** [ICT operations manager](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/f0ca39a8-1e30-487c-95f9-ef2d5e734adc) (ISCO-08 1330)

## Summary

Problem managers find and fix the root causes of incidents, so that faults stop happening again. They analyse incident data, run root cause investigations, record known errors and workarounds, and track fixes with technical teams and suppliers. In health care, repeated faults in clinical systems waste clinical time, erode trust, and can create patient safety risks, so removing them matters.

## In a digital health care organisation

- Repeated small faults, such as slow logins or failed prints on wards, add up to lost clinical time, so problem managers look for patterns as well as major failures.
- Some problems create clinical safety hazards, such as results not reaching the right clinician, and are assessed with the clinical safety officer.
- Root cause investigations often involve suppliers of clinical systems, and are managed through contracts and service levels.
- Workarounds for clinical systems must be safe and clearly explained to clinical staff.

## UK GDaD PCF role description

> Problem managers aim to resolve and control the root causes of incidents caused by errors within the IT infrastructure. In this role, you will be expected to work to prevent the recurrence of these incidents.

## Band 5: Problem analyst

**UK GDaD PCF level: Problem analyst**

> At this level, you will be responsible for maintaining software systems once they are up and running and, where necessary, support the problem manager in co-ordinating the resolution of any problems.

### Responsibilities

- Analyse incident data to find repeated faults and trends across clinical and corporate services.
- Record problems, known errors, and workarounds in the service management tool.
- Support root cause investigations by gathering logs, timelines, and other evidence.
- Track actions with technical teams and suppliers until fixes are in place.
- Check that workarounds for clinical systems are safe and clearly explained to users.

## Band 6: Problem manager

**UK GDaD PCF level: Problem manager**

> A problem manager picks up feeds from numerous sources, such as the service desk, with the aim of putting in place continual improvements to stop incidents such as regular service outages recurring.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - resolve emerging and recurring problems
> - perform root cause analysis to minimise the adverse impact of incidents caused by errors within the IT infrastructure
> - work closely with major incident managers
> - deal with problems and root cause analysis

### Responsibilities

- Run the problem management process across the organisation's services.
- Lead root cause analysis after major incidents and for recurring faults, using structured techniques.
- Prioritise problems by their effect on patient care, staff time, and service risk.
- Assess problems that may create patient safety hazards with the clinical safety officer.
- Hold technical teams and suppliers to account for permanent fixes, and report progress to service leaders.
- Work with major incident managers to make sure lessons from incidents lead to change.

## ESCO occupations and skills

### ICT operations manager

- **URI:** <http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/f0ca39a8-1e30-487c-95f9-ef2d5e734adc>
- **ESCO code:** 1330.5  ·  **ISCO-08:** 1330 Information and communications technology service managers
- **Alternative labels:** ICT data processing manager; data processing manager; ICT operations managers; IT data processing manager; IT process manager; IT operations manager; systems and networks manager; ICT acquisitions manager

> ICT operations managers coordinate ICT services and infrastructure ensuring that the organisation has the required infrastructural resources. They also plan and monitor stages of either a business process or a computer process, negotiate contracts and take mitigation action in case of non-fulfilment of agreements. They oversee the day-to-day tasks involving infrastructure components, ICT systems and software.
