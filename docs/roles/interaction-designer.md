# Interaction designer

> This is an illustrative reference profile for a generic digital health care organisation. It is not an official job description for any employer, and its job evaluation scores are not a formal evaluation.

**Family:** [User-centred design](../index.md#user-centred-design)  
**Bands:** 4, 5, 6, 7, 8b, 8c  
**UK GDaD PCF role:** [Interaction designer](https://understand-digital-data-roles-skills.service.gov.uk/role/interaction-designer/)  
**ESCO occupations:** [user interface designer](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/96e20037-0a25-4bf6-a25e-808d1605d890) (ISCO-08 2513)

## Summary

Interaction designers design how patients, carers, clinicians, and care staff use the organisation's digital health services, from patient-facing apps and forms to the screens clinicians use at the bedside. They design simple, accessible, and safe interactions, test them with real users, and use shared design systems so that services feel consistent and familiar.

## In a digital health care organisation

- Clinicians often use systems under time pressure, with interruptions and on shared devices, so designs must support fast, accurate work and make the safe action the easy one.
- Poor design can cause clinical harm, such as selecting the wrong patient or misreading a dose, so designers apply human factors principles and work with clinical safety officers.
- Many users are unwell, anxious, older, or disabled, so designs must meet accessibility standards and work for people with low digital skills or health literacy.
- Alerts and warnings are only useful if people notice and trust them, so designers help reduce alert fatigue and keep safety-critical information visible.

## UK GDaD PCF role description

> An interaction designer works out the best way to let users interact with services, in terms of both overall flow and at the level of individual design elements.

## Band 4: Associate interaction designer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Associate interaction designer**

> As a trainee in an entry-level position, working under supervision, you need aptitude, potential and an understanding of the role.

### Responsibilities

- Learn interaction design methods and the team's design system through training and pairing.
- Create sketches, wireframes, and simple prototypes under supervision.
- Help test designs with users and record what works and what does not.
- Follow accessibility standards and the design system's patterns, including those for patient identity and safety warnings.

## Band 5: Junior interaction designer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Junior interaction designer**

> A junior interaction designer is a graduate with a degree in a relevant subject or an individual with some relevant work experience, or both.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - explain design decisions
> - work collaboratively
> - have a responsibility as part of a service
> - work independently after being given direction by more senior designers
> - independently identify user issues and important needs

### Responsibilities

- Design and prototype parts of a service, such as forms, flows, and screens, with direction from a more senior designer.
- Test designs with patients, carers, and staff, and change them based on what you learn.
- Design accessible interactions that work with assistive technologies.
- Explain design decisions to the team, including how they reduce the chance of error.

## Band 6: Interaction designer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Interaction designer**

> An interaction designer is a confident and competent designer who can develop designs based on evidence of user needs and organisational outcomes.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - be trusted to make good decisions
> - recognise when to ask for further guidance and support
> - contribute to the development of design concepts
> - interpret evidence-based research and incorporate this into your work

### Responsibilities

- Design interactions for a digital health product, from early concepts to detailed, tested designs.
- Design for clinical workflows, testing with clinicians and care staff in realistic scenarios, including interruptions.
- Raise design-related hazards with the clinical safety officer and record how designs control them.
- Use and contribute to the design system, including patterns for safety-critical information.
- Work closely with developers to make sure designs are built as intended and remain accessible.

## Band 7: Senior interaction designer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Senior interaction designer**

> A senior interaction designer is an experienced designer who works with minimal support and can influence and mentor others.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - work with service managers and programme directors to develop design concepts
> - potentially have responsibility across complex services
> - help set direction and embed good practice within teams
> - make important decisions based on research and understand how this research impacts others

### Responsibilities

- Lead interaction design for complex services, such as clinical systems used across many care settings.
- Apply human factors methods, such as task analysis, to safety-critical clinical workflows.
- Provide design evidence for clinical safety cases and, where software is regulated, for usability files.
- Help set direction and good practice for design in teams, and mentor other designers.
- Make design decisions from research and data, and explain them to clinical and product leaders.

## Band 8b: Lead interaction designer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Lead interaction designer**

> A lead interaction designer is an expert practitioner who influences and mentors others.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - work with service managers and programme directors to develop design concepts
> - set direction and assure the quality of design delivery across teams
> - lead multiple or highly complex services

### Responsibilities

- Set design direction and assure the quality of interaction design across several teams or services.
- Lead the design system, including accessible and safety-critical patterns that teams across the organisation reuse.
- Lead human factors work on the highest-risk clinical services, with clinical safety officers.
- Work with senior product and clinical leaders to develop design concepts for major services.
- Line manage and develop interaction designers.

## Band 8c: Head of interaction design

**UK GDaD PCF level: Head of interaction design**

> A head of interaction design is an expert practitioner with broad industry experience, who can define and assure best practice while influencing, leading and mentoring others.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - influence both design and organisational strategy and priorities
> - collaborate with counterpart colleagues across government
> - focus on ensuring the right conditions and environment for designers to work effectively

### Responsibilities

- Lead the interaction design profession and set its strategy, standards, and ways of working.
- Influence design and organisational strategy so that services are usable, accessible, and safe.
- Own the organisation's design system and make sure it meets accessibility and clinical safety needs.
- Build design capability, including recruitment, career paths, and training.
- Work with peers across the health and care system on shared design patterns.
