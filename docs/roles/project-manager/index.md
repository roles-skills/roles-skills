# Project manager

> This is an illustrative reference profile for a generic digital health care organisation. It is not an official job description for any employer, and its job evaluation scores are not a formal evaluation.

**Family:** [Product and delivery](../../#product-and-delivery)  
**Bands:** 5, 6, 7, 8a, 8b  
**UK GDaD PCF role:** none (this reference defines the role)  
**ESCO occupations:** [project manager](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/bea99fea-0383-4c63-b944-70d4799de2c5) (ISCO-08 1219); [ICT project manager](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/8b6388a4-4904-471b-9331-d3b1211f5525) (ISCO-08 1330)

## Summary

Project managers plan and deliver projects that change how the organisation supports health and care, such as deploying a clinical system to new sites, moving services to new infrastructure, or introducing new digital tools for care staff. They control scope, time, cost, quality, and risk, using recognised project methods, and make sure projects deliver the benefits they promised for patients and staff.

## In a digital health care organisation

- Projects that change clinical systems or workflows need clinical safety assurance before go-live, so project managers plan hazard workshops, safety case sign-off, and clinical safety officer input into the schedule.
- Go-lives must not disrupt patient care, so project managers plan cutovers, business continuity arrangements, and floor-walking support with clinical and operational teams.
- Projects often need data protection impact assessments and information sharing agreements, so project managers plan these early with information governance colleagues.
- Benefits in care, such as staff time released or fewer errors, take time to appear, so project managers set up baselines and hand over benefits tracking to the service.

## Band 5: Assistant project manager

An assistant project manager supports the delivery of projects, or manages small, well-defined projects with guidance from a more senior project manager.

### Responsibilities

- Maintain project plans, risk and issue logs, action logs, and dependency records.
- Organise and minute project meetings and boards, and follow up actions.
- Manage small, well-defined pieces of work, such as a site rollout or a training schedule, with guidance.
- Track readiness tasks for go-live, such as clinical safety sign-off, data protection checks, and staff training.
- Prepare highlight reports and track project spending against budget.

## Band 6: Project manager

A project manager runs projects of moderate size and complexity from start to close, such as rolling out a digital service to several care settings.

### Responsibilities

- Plan and manage projects from start-up to closure, controlling scope, time, cost, quality, and risk.
- Plan go-lives with clinical and operational teams, including cutover, business continuity, and support arrangements.
- Make sure clinical safety, information governance, and accessibility assurance is planned and completed before go-live.
- Manage the project budget and supplier deliverables.
- Report to the project board and escalate risks and issues outside tolerance.
- Set baselines for benefits and hand over benefits tracking to the service.

## Band 7: Senior project manager

A senior project manager leads large, complex, or high-risk projects, such as replacing a clinical system across an organisation, and may manage other project staff.

### Responsibilities

- Lead large or high-risk projects, such as clinical system replacements or multi-site deployments.
- Manage complex dependencies with suppliers, other projects, and partner health and care organisations.
- Lead go-live decisions with the clinical safety officer, operational leads, and the senior responsible owner.
- Manage the project budget and commercial arrangements, including change control with suppliers.
- Make sure benefits for patients and staff are defined, owned, and measured.
- Manage and develop project managers and project support staff.

## Band 8a: Lead project manager

A lead project manager leads the most complex projects or a group of related projects, and sets project management practice for their area.

### Responsibilities

- Lead the most complex projects, or a group of related projects, across several services or organisations.
- Set project management standards, templates, and assurance for the area, and coach project managers.
- Advise senior responsible owners and boards on delivery options, risks, and recovery of struggling projects.
- Manage significant budgets and contracts, and make sure projects give value for money.
- Make sure clinical safety and information governance requirements are met across the group of projects.
- Line manage project managers.

## Band 8b: Head of project management

A head of project management leads the organisation's project management practice and the project managers who deliver its digital change.

### Responsibilities

- Lead the project management practice, including its methods, standards, tools, and assurance.

- Build project management capability, including recruitment, training, and career paths.
- Advise executive leaders on project health, delivery risks, and lessons learned.
- Manage the practice's budget.

> Project managers oversee the project on a daily basis and are responsible for delivering high-quality results within the identified objectives and constraints, ensuring the effective use of the allocated resources.
