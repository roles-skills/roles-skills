## Summary

Infrastructure engineers design, build, run, and support the networks, servers, storage, cloud platforms, and hosting that the organisation's digital services depend on. They keep this infrastructure available, secure, and recoverable, and work with suppliers and architects to change it safely. In health care, one infrastructure failure can stop clinical systems across hospitals and community services at once.

## In a digital health care organisation

- Clinical systems need very high availability, so infrastructure is designed for resilience and changes are planned around clinical activity.
- Engineers take part in on-call rotas, because infrastructure faults at night or at weekends can stop patient care.
- Networks connect medical devices, such as infusion pumps, patient monitors, and imaging equipment, which need network segmentation and careful change following IEC 80001-1.
- Health care is a frequent target for ransomware, so backups must be protected, tested, and restorable within agreed recovery times.
- Infrastructure often mixes old and new technology, including legacy clinical systems that cannot easily be patched, so engineers put compensating controls in place.

## Band 4: Associate infrastructure engineer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Associate infrastructure engineer**

> As a trainee, you will work in an established team and support infrastructure engineers in their daily work, under their direction.

### Responsibilities

- Support infrastructure engineers with routine tasks, such as server builds, backup checks, and network changes.
- Monitor infrastructure and report alerts and faults to the right team.
- Follow change, security, and information governance procedures in all work.
- Record configuration and asset details accurately.
- Work towards an infrastructure apprenticeship or equivalent qualification.

## Band 6: Infrastructure engineer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Infrastructure engineer**

> Working in a team of infrastructure specialists and engineers, an infrastructure engineer supports and maintains infrastructure solutions and services as directed and according to departmental policy.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - ensure services are delivered and used as required
> - work with and support third parties to provide infrastructure services

### Responsibilities

- Build, configure, and maintain the servers, storage, networks, and cloud services that host clinical and corporate systems.
- Patch infrastructure promptly, balancing security risk against the need to keep clinical systems available.
- Run and test backups and restores, including for critical clinical systems.
- Support segmented networks for medical devices and other clinical equipment, working with clinical engineering teams.
- Investigate and fix infrastructure faults, including as part of the on-call rota.
- Automate routine tasks using scripts and infrastructure as code.

## Band 7: Senior infrastructure engineer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Senior infrastructure engineer**

> Working in a team of infrastructure specialists and engineers, a senior infrastructure engineer builds, administers, supports and maintains solutions as directed and according to departmental policy.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - ensure services are integrated, delivered and operated as required
> - work with and support third parties in providing infrastructure services
> - work with technical architects to translate architectural designs into operations
> - provide input into overall management - workforce, budget, technology roadmaps, projects and tasks
> - lead and direct infrastructure specialist teams in building, managing, supporting and maintaining solutions according to departmental policy (if taking a managerial path)

### Responsibilities

- Design and build infrastructure solutions from architects' designs, with the resilience that clinical services need.
- Plan and lead major infrastructure changes, such as data centre moves, network refreshes, and cloud migrations, around clinical activity.
- Design and test disaster recovery and ransomware recovery for critical clinical systems.
- Lead the technical response to infrastructure incidents and drive fixes for their causes.
- Manage compensating controls for legacy systems and medical devices that cannot be patched.
- Lead and coach infrastructure engineers.

## Band 8b: Principal infrastructure engineer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Principal infrastructure engineer**

> A principal infrastructure engineer is an accomplished technical leader.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - develop strategic and tactical engineering roadmaps for technologies and services in your area of responsibility, ensuring they're future-proofed and that the organisation derives maximum value from investment in technologies

- **Alternative labels:** hybrid cloud engineer; cloud infrastructure engineer; cloud software engineer; cloud computing engineer; cloud network engineer; cloud solution engineer; cloud security engineer; cloud-native engineer; cloud developer
