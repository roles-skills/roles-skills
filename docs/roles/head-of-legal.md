# Head of legal services

> This is an illustrative reference profile for a generic digital health care organisation. It is not an official job description for any employer, and its job evaluation scores are not a formal evaluation.

**Family:** [Legal](../index.md#legal)  
**Bands:** 8c, 8d, 9  
**UK GDaD PCF role:** none (this reference defines the role)  
**ESCO occupations:** [lawyer](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/974d4eab-fbee-4c52-b16e-73bdd8c25b53) (ISCO-08 2611); [legal consultant](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/31854d78-3615-43a4-aa3a-c597ecb20ceb) (ISCO-08 2619)

## Summary

Senior legal leaders make sure the organisation acts lawfully and manages its legal risk well. They lead the in-house legal team, manage external legal advisers, and advise the board and executive on the organisation's most significant legal matters. In a digital health care organisation this includes the legal basis for using health data, major technology and data contracts, intellectual property in software, and the organisation's statutory duties.

## In a digital health care organisation

- The organisation is a public body or works with them, so its decisions can be challenged, and it must act within its powers.
- The use of health and care data at scale, including for AI and research, carries high legal and reputational risk.
- Major technology contracts are long, valuable, and hard to exit, so their legal terms shape the organisation for years.
- Legal leaders work closely with the board, the data protection officer, clinical safety, and information governance colleagues.

## Band 8c: Deputy head of legal services

Deputy heads of legal services lead a major area of the legal function, such as commercial, technology, and data law, and deputise for the head of legal services.

### Responsibilities

- Lead a major area of legal work, such as commercial, technology, and data law, or disputes and employment.
- Advise directors on high-risk matters, including major procurements and data programmes.
- Manage the panel of external lawyers and the budget for external legal advice.
- Set legal standards, templates, and training for the organisation.
- Lead and develop several legal teams or specialists.
- Deputise for the head of legal services.

## Band 8d: Head of legal services

Heads of legal services lead the organisation's legal function, set its approach to legal risk, and advise the executive and board.

### Responsibilities

- Lead the in-house legal function and the use of external lawyers.
- Advise the executive and board on legal risk, statutory duties, and major decisions.
- Set the organisation's legal risk appetite and positions on key issues, such as data use and contracting.
- Lead legal work on the most significant matters, such as major disputes, public inquiries, and partnerships.
- Hold the budget for legal services and show value for money.
- Lead and develop the legal team and legal operations.

## Band 9: General counsel

General counsel are the organisation's most senior lawyer, with corporate responsibility for legal services and legal risk, and often also lead corporate governance as board secretary.

### Responsibilities

- Hold corporate responsibility for the organisation's legal affairs and legal risk.
- Advise the board and chair on the organisation's powers, duties, and governance.
- Make sure major strategies, partnerships, and data programmes are lawful.
- Lead the response to the most serious legal threats, such as judicial reviews and regulatory action.
- Lead legal services, and often corporate governance and the board secretariat.
- Represent the organisation with regulators, partners, and government bodies.

### legal consultant

- **URI:** <http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/31854d78-3615-43a4-aa3a-c597ecb20ceb>
- **ESCO code:** 2619.7  ·  **ISCO-08:** 2619 Legal professionals not elsewhere classified
- **Alternative labels:** business law consultant; legal advisor; city legal adviser; legal adviser; city legal advisor; legal nurse consultant

> Legal consultants advise a varied array of clients ranging from business, individuals, up to legal firms. They provide legal advisory outside courtrooms in relation to legal affairs which depend on the nature of the client. They advise in matters such as merging of multinationals, housing purchase, modification of contracts and its implications. They help clients in general to abide by the regulations and avoid committing illegal acts.
