# Human resources business partner

> This is an illustrative reference profile for a generic digital health care organisation. It is not an official job description for any employer, and its job evaluation scores are not a formal evaluation.

**Family:** [Human resources](../index.md#human-resources)  
**Bands:** 7, 8a, 8b  
**UK GDaD PCF role:** none (this reference defines the role)  
**ESCO occupations:** [human resources manager](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/f605bcd2-90b6-45a0-a558-d05016d68a77) (ISCO-08 1212); [human resources officer](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/d3e32e5e-7f24-48e3-b939-e4f800eb62fb) (ISCO-08 2423)

## Summary

HR business partners work alongside directorate leaders to make sure the organisation has the people, skills, structures, and culture it needs to deliver its services. They turn the people strategy into plans for each directorate, lead organisational change, and give senior advice on the most complex people issues. In a digital health care organisation much of this is about building and keeping digital, data, and technology capability.

## In a digital health care organisation

- Digital, data, and technology skills are scarce, so business partners lead workforce plans that combine recruitment, apprenticeships, reskilling, and retention, and use skills frameworks such as this reference for gap analysis.
- Many services run 24/7, so changes to rotas, on-call, and service models need careful consultation and attention to staff wellbeing and safe staffing.
- Digital roles are often new or fast-changing, so business partners make sure job descriptions are evaluated consistently and banded fairly against similar roles.
- Organisational change often involves clinical and care staff as well as digital teams, so business partners work with clinical leaders and staff representatives.
- People risks, such as reliance on contractors or a small number of experts, can become risks to patient-facing services.

## Band 7: HR business partner

Is the senior HR adviser to the leadership team of a directorate, leading its people plan, organisational change, and complex employee relations, within the organisation's people strategy.

### Responsibilities

- Advise the directorate leadership team on people risks, priorities, and plans.
- Lead the directorate's workforce plan, using skills gap analysis against this reference to plan recruitment, development, and new roles.
- Lead organisational change, such as restructures and new service models, from business case through formal consultation to implementation.
- Oversee complex and high-risk employee relations cases and advise senior managers and panels.
- Use workforce data, such as turnover, vacancies, agency spend, and staff survey results, to challenge and support leaders.
- Advise on banding and reward for new and changing roles, including recruitment and retention premiums.
- Lead and develop HR advisers who support the directorate.

- A recognised HR professional qualification at advanced level, or equivalent experience.

- **Leadership:** Leads a team or a professional practice area.

## Band 8a: Senior HR business partner

Business partners several directorates, or the largest and most complex ones, such as digital and technology, and leads people programmes that reach across the organisation.

### Responsibilities

- Advise executive and senior leaders of several directorates on people strategy, risk, and organisational design.
- Lead large or sensitive organisational changes, including service transfers and changes to 24/7 working patterns.
- Lead an organisation-wide people programme, such as a digital careers framework or a retention plan for scarce skills.
- Handle the most sensitive cases, such as those involving senior staff or a risk of legal claims, with legal advisers.
- Shape people policies and consult on them with staff representatives.
- Lead, coach, and develop HR business partners and advisers.

## Band 8b: Head of HR business partnering

Leads the HR business partnering service across the organisation and makes sure every directorate has a people plan that delivers the people strategy.

### Responsibilities

- Lead and manage the HR business partner and HR adviser teams.
- Make sure every directorate has a workforce and people plan linked to its service and financial plans.
- Lead the organisation's approach to organisational change, consultation, and partnership working with staff representatives.
- Report people risks and workforce performance to executive committees.
- Hold the budget for the business partnering service.
- Work with leaders of recruitment, learning, and organisational development to deliver joined-up people services.

> Human resources managers plan, design and implement processes related to the human capital of companies. They develop programs for recruiting, interviewing, and selecting employees based on a previous assessment of the profile and skills required in the company. Moreover, they manage compensation and development programs for the company's employees comprising trainings, skill assessment and yearly evaluations, promotion, expat programs, and general assurance of the well-being of the employees in the workplace.

### human resources officer

- **URI:** <http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/d3e32e5e-7f24-48e3-b939-e4f800eb62fb>
- **ESCO code:** 2423.3  ·  **ISCO-08:** 2423 Personnel and careers professionals
- **Alternative labels:** personnel administrator; personnel supervisor; personnel manager; HR officer; personnel development specialist; personnel representative; HR supervisor; skills trainer; personnel director; HR worker; HR representative; personnel expert; personnel assistant; human resources representative; human resources supervisor; personnel rep; human resource officer; personnel recruiter; HR director; HR rep; human resources worker; personnel officer; personnel worker; competencies trainer; human resources rep; human resources director; lifelong learning coach

> Human resources officers develop and implement strategies that help their employers select and retain appropriately qualified staff within that business sector. They recruit staff, prepare job advertisements, interview and short-list people, negotiate with employment agencies, and set up working conditions. Human resources officers also administer the payroll, review salaries and advise on remuneration benefits and employment law. They arrange for training opportunities to enhance employees' performance.
