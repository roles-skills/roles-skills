# Software developer

> This is an illustrative reference profile for a generic digital health care organisation. It is not an official job description for any employer, and its job evaluation scores are not a formal evaluation.

**Family:** [Software development](../index.md#software-development)  
**Bands:** 4, 5, 6, 7, 8a, 8b  
**UK GDaD PCF role:** [Software developer](https://understand-digital-data-roles-skills.service.gov.uk/role/software-developer/)  
**ESCO occupations:** [software developer](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/f2b15a0e-e65a-438a-affb-29b9d50b77d1) (ISCO-08 2512); [ICT application developer](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/bd272aee-adc9-4a06-a15c-a73b4b4a46a7) (ISCO-08 2514)

## Summary

Software developers build, test, and maintain the software behind the organisation's digital health services, such as patient-facing apps, clinical systems, integration services, and data platforms. They work in multidisciplinary product teams, write code that is safe, secure, accessible, and maintainable, and support their services in live operation.

## In a digital health care organisation

- Software can affect patient care, so developers follow the clinical risk management process and contribute evidence to safety cases.
- Code often handles confidential health information, so developers apply information governance and secure coding practices by default.
- Many services exchange data with other health and care systems, using open interoperability standards such as HL7 FHIR.
- Some software may be regulated as a medical device, so developers need to know when to ask for regulatory advice.

## UK GDaD PCF role description

> A software developer designs, runs and improves software that meets user needs.
> 
> In this role, you will:
> - be responsible for writing clean, secure code following a test-driven approach
> - create code that is open by default and easy for others to reuse

## Band 4: Apprentice software developer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Apprentice developer**

> An apprentice developer attends certified training and develops skills on the job.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - spend a substantial portion of time shadowing others
> - work with other developers to write code and tests
> - build your knowledge
> - follow a test-driven approach
> - write code that is automatically tested

### Responsibilities

- Learn the team's languages, tools, and ways of working through structured training and pairing.
- Write and test small, well-defined pieces of code under close supervision.
- Follow secure coding, information governance, and clinical safety procedures.
- Take part in team ceremonies such as stand-ups, planning, and retrospectives.
- Work towards an apprenticeship or equivalent qualification.

## Band 5: Junior software developer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Junior developer**

> A junior developer learns on the job by delivering software components.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - develop skills while working under supervision to deliver stories in a multidisciplinary team
> - understand and be proficient in different types of testing
> - be aware of but not responsible for security
> - coach and mentor more junior colleagues

### Responsibilities

- Build and test features for the team's products, working from user stories with guidance.
- Write automated tests and fix defects, including in services that support patient care.
- Contribute evidence to clinical safety cases, such as test results and change records.
- Handle health data securely, following information governance rules.
- Support live services, under guidance, as part of the team's support arrangements.

## Band 6: Software developer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Developer**

> A developer delivers software components that form part of a product.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - develop software to meet user needs
> - follow best practice guidelines and help to improve those guidelines
> - write clean, secure and well-tested code
> - coach and mentor more junior colleagues
> - operate the services you build and identify issues in production

### Responsibilities

- Design, build, and test features and services independently, choosing suitable approaches.
- Review code and help maintain the team's engineering standards.
- Build integrations with other health and care systems using open standards such as HL7 FHIR.
- Take part in hazard identification and help put clinical safety controls into code and tests.
- Investigate and fix problems in live services, including out-of-hours support where the service needs it.
- Mentor junior and apprentice developers.

## Band 7: Senior software developer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Senior developer**

> A senior developer delivers and integrates software to form a complete service.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - plan and lead development on sets of related stories
> - have an understanding of the whole system and take responsibility for teaching this to others
> - work with other disciplines to understand what needs to be built
> - coach and mentor more junior colleagues
> - operate the production services you build
> - find ways to improve system robustness, resilience and stability
> There are 2 different roles at this level - a technical role and a management role.

### Responsibilities

- Lead the technical design and delivery of significant features and services.
- Set and maintain engineering standards for the team, including testing, security, and accessibility.
- Make technical decisions that balance user needs, clinical safety, security, and cost.
- Lead incident investigation for the team's services and drive the fixes.
- Work with clinical safety officers to make sure safety controls are designed in and tested.
- Coach and develop developers in the team.

## Band 8a: Lead software developer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Lead developer**

> A lead developer leads development on one or a small number of related project teams.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - guide the ways in which the team works
> - be proficient in a wide range of technical systems
> - be involved in identifying appropriate technology and approaches, deciding when software should be written
> - contribute to the developer community
> - provide technical leadership, coaching and mentoring your team
> - promote knowledge sharing and adoption of good practice
> There are 2 different roles at this level - a technical role and a management role.

### Responsibilities

- Lead the engineering for a product or group of teams, setting technical direction.
- Make sure the software is designed for safety, security, resilience, and interoperability.
- Work with architects, product managers, and clinical leads to shape the product roadmap.
- Lead the development community of practice and set standards across teams.
- Line manage or lead developers, supporting their development and performance.

## Band 8b: Principal software developer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Principal developer**

> A principal developer leads and plans development across large or multiple teams.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - be an expert in many technical areas or a specialist with very deep knowledge in a particular technical area
> - use this knowledge, and work with related disciplines, to keep multiple teams working effectively
> - develop capability by facilitating internal mobility, shaping career paths and recruiting talent, ensuring they collaborate
> - identify, test and champion the adoption of emerging technologies
> There are 2 different roles at this level - a technical role and a management role.

### Responsibilities

- Set the engineering strategy, standards, and practices for the organisation's software development.
- Advise senior leaders on technology choices, risks, and investment.
- Lead the resolution of the most complex technical problems across services.
- Represent the organisation in cross-organisation and open source engineering communities.
- Lead and develop lead developers and grow the organisation's engineering capability.
