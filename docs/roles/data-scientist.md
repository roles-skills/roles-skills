## Summary

Data scientists use statistics, machine learning, and other advanced methods to find patterns in health and care data and to build models that help services plan, predict, and improve care. Typical work includes forecasting demand, identifying patients who may benefit from early support, and evaluating the effect of service changes. They work closely with clinicians and analysts, and make sure models are valid, fair, and used safely.

## In a digital health care organisation

- A model that supports decisions about patients can cause harm if it is wrong or biased, so models are validated on the population they will be used with and assessed for clinical safety.
- Health data reflects unequal access to care, so models can repeat or worsen health inequalities unless bias is measured and addressed for each patient group.
- Some models, such as those that inform diagnosis or treatment, may be regulated as medical devices, and AI regulation may also apply.
- Data science often needs linked, detailed patient data, so work happens in secure settings, with pseudonymised data, under an agreed information governance basis.
- Clinicians and patients need to understand and trust a model's outputs, so explanation and plain-language communication of uncertainty matter.

## UK GDaD PCF role description

> Data science is a broad and fast-moving field spanning maths, statistics, software engineering and communications. Data scientists will often work as part of a multidisciplinary team, using data and analytics to inform and achieve organisational goals.
> 
> In this role, you will:
> - be inquisitive
> - explore and visualise data
> - make recommendations to address complex problems and to inform strategic and operational decision making
> - use data ethically and appropriately
> - be innovative and adaptable
> - explore existing and new data using a range of statistical tools and techniques, such as machine learning and predictive analytics
> - find patterns in data and transform them into organisational insight

## Band 4: Trainee data scientist

**UK GDaD PCF level: Trainee data scientist**

> A trainee data scientist is inquisitive, curious about data and keen to learn and develop.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - have a basic understanding of software development and analytical methods
> - interpret statistical outputs effectively
> - be aware of how data science techniques can be used, and have a basic knowledge of those key techniques, such as machine learning
> - work as part of a multidisciplinary team to develop data science solutions and outputs
> - prepare and manipulate data
> - present effectively
> - be aware of ethical considerations
> - be aware of the technologies used

### Responsibilities

- Learn the team's statistical and machine learning methods, tools, and secure ways of working through structured training.
- Prepare and explore pseudonymised health data sets under supervision.
- Run and interpret simple statistical analyses, checking results with a more experienced data scientist.
- Follow information governance, disclosure control, and ethics procedures.
- Work towards a data science apprenticeship or equivalent qualification.

> - understand the basics of software development and analytical methods, and be able to develop basic models
> - understand and be able to implement a range of data science techniques, including machine learning
> - collaborate with others to develop data science solutions and outputs supporting the organisation
> - prepare and manipulate data, and perform analytics
> - present and communicate effectively
> - be aware of project delivery methods
> - be aware of ethical considerations

## Band 6: Data scientist

**UK GDaD PCF level: Data scientist**

> A data scientist will work independently and have a good understanding of a range of topics, including data science techniques, delivery methods and stages (such as minimal viable products), tools and technologies.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - develop complex solutions using a range of data science techniques, whilst understanding any ethical considerations
> - understand the role and benefits of data science within the organisation
> - support capability building within the organisation
> - collaborate with others to develop data science solutions and outputs supporting the organisation
> - prepare and manipulate data, and perform complex analytics
> - present and communicate effectively

### Responsibilities

- Develop complex models independently, such as risk stratification or length-of-stay prediction, choosing suitable methods.
- Validate models with clinicians, checking that they are accurate, fair, and useful on local patient data.
- Document each model's intended use, data, performance, and limits, and contribute evidence to clinical safety cases.
- Evaluate the effect of service changes using suitable statistical methods.
- Explain models and their uncertainty to clinical and non-technical audiences.
- Support associate and trainee data scientists.

## Band 7: Principal data scientist

**UK GDaD PCF level: Principal data scientist**

> A principal data scientist is a leader of data science, quite often with responsibility for managing and developing teams.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - have a broad knowledge of data science techniques, use cases and potential impact, as well as the tools and technologies
> - have extensive experience in scoping, designing and delivering data science outputs and products
> - work collaboratively with a range of experts in support of organisational objectives
> - communicate effectively and challenge delivery plans and priorities
> - appreciate and understand data ethics, data preparation and manipulation
> - appreciate and understand delivery methods, and how to deliver supported solutions at scale

### Responsibilities

- Scope, design, and lead data science projects that support service planning and patient care.
- Decide which methods, tools, and data are suitable, and challenge plans that are not feasible or safe.
- Plan the validation, bias assessment, and monitoring of models with clinical safety and information governance colleagues.
- Agree with product teams how models will be deployed and supported at scale.
- Manage and develop a small team of data scientists.
