# User researcher

> This is an illustrative reference profile for a generic digital health care organisation. It is not an official job description for any employer, and its job evaluation scores are not a formal evaluation.

**Family:** [User-centred design](../../#user-centred-design)  
**Bands:** 4, 5, 6, 7, 8b, 8c  
**UK GDaD PCF role:** [User researcher](https://understand-digital-data-roles-skills.service.gov.uk/role/user-researcher/)  
**ESCO occupations:** [user experience analyst](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/faed411a-f920-4100-86a8-b877928b429c) (ISCO-08 2511)

## Summary

User researchers help teams understand the people who use and deliver health and care services, including patients, carers, clinicians, and care staff. They plan and carry out research safely and ethically, turn what they learn into clear insight, and make sure decisions about digital health services are based on evidence of real needs, including the needs of people who find services hardest to use.

## In a digital health care organisation

- Research participants are often unwell, anxious, or in vulnerable circumstances, so researchers plan carefully for informed consent, safeguarding, and participants' wellbeing.
- Research may hear about distressing experiences of illness, loss, or poor care, so researchers need support to manage the emotional effect on themselves and their team.
- Clinicians and care staff have little time away from patients, so researchers plan research in clinical settings without disrupting care, often through observation and short sessions.
- Some research counts as formal health research or uses patient data, so researchers need to know when ethics or research governance approval and data protection checks apply.
- Health inequalities are real, so researchers make deliberate efforts to include people with low digital skills, disabilities, or limited English, and people who use services least.

## UK GDaD PCF role description

> User researchers plan, design and carry out research activities with users that help teams get a deep understanding of the people that use government services. This research informs policy, proposition, service, content and interaction design so that services work well for users and achieve policy intent.

## Band 4: Associate user researcher

**UK GDaD PCF level: Associate user researcher**

> As a trainee in an entry-level role, working with more experienced user researchers, you will need to have an understanding of the role and show potential, although you will need guidance and training to produce good work and develop your skills.

### Responsibilities

- Learn user research methods through training and by working alongside experienced researchers.
- Take notes, help run sessions, and help recruit participants, following consent and safeguarding procedures.
- Store research data securely and in line with information governance rules.
- Help analyse research data and share findings with the team.

## Band 5: Junior user researcher

**UK GDaD PCF level: Junior user researcher**

> A junior user researcher is embedded in a multidisciplinary team to carry out user research activities.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - have some practical experience but need regular guidance and training to produce your best work and develop your skills
> - work in combination with a more senior user researcher

### Responsibilities

- Plan and run research sessions with patients, carers, and staff, with guidance from a more senior researcher.
- Use methods such as interviews, usability testing, and contextual observation in care settings.
- Get informed consent, adapt sessions for participants' needs, and raise any safeguarding concerns.
- Analyse findings and present them to the team as clear, actionable insight.
- Recruit diverse participants, including people who find services hard to use.

## Band 6: User researcher

**UK GDaD PCF level: User researcher**

> A user researcher is usually embedded in a multidisciplinary team and responsible for planning and carrying out user research activities. At this level, you will be expected to work independently on a team.

### Responsibilities

- Plan and carry out user research for a multidisciplinary team, choosing methods that fit the questions and the participants.
- Research with clinicians and care staff in their work settings to understand clinical workflows and safety-critical tasks.
- Plan research with people in vulnerable circumstances safely, and judge when ethics or research governance approval is needed.
- Share insight that helps the team prioritise, including evidence about safety and accessibility.
- Bring the team into research so they understand users first-hand.

## Band 7: Senior user researcher

**UK GDaD PCF level: Senior user researcher**

> A senior user researcher is an experienced practitioner who can plan and lead user research activities in larger teams and on more complex services.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - build user-centred practices in new teams
> - align user research activities with wider plans to inform a service proposition
> - supervise and develop other user researchers to assure and improve research practice

### Responsibilities

- Plan and lead research for complex services, such as those that span several care settings or serve people in crisis.
- Align research with the service's wider plans and with other teams' research.
- Plan safe research with people in vulnerable circumstances, and prepare ethics and research governance submissions.
- Supervise and develop user researchers, and assure the quality and ethics of their work.
- Build user-centred practice in new teams and with clinical stakeholders.

## Band 8b: Lead user researcher

**UK GDaD PCF level: Lead user researcher**

> A lead user researcher is an expert practitioner, leading and aligning user research activities across several teams.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - ensure that teams take a user-centred, evidence-based approach to service design and delivery
> - develop and assure good user research practice

### Responsibilities

- Lead and align user research across several teams or a whole programme.
- Make sure teams take a user-centred, evidence-based approach, and that research reaches people most affected by health inequalities.
- Set and assure research practice, including consent, safeguarding, data handling, and participant wellbeing.
- Build shared research assets, such as research repositories and participant panels, that teams can reuse safely.
- Line manage and develop user researchers.

## Band 8c: Head of user research

**UK GDaD PCF level: Head of user research**

> A head of user research leads user researchers in an organisation and attracts and builds talent.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - be an expert practitioner who can define and assure best practice
> - influence organisational strategy and priorities
> - collaborate with colleagues across government

### Responsibilities

- Lead the user research profession and set its strategy, standards, and ethics.
- Influence organisational strategy and priorities with evidence about patients, carers, and care staff.
- Attract, build, and develop research talent, including career paths and recruitment.
- Assure research practice across the organisation and act as the escalation point for high-risk research.
- Represent research practice with ethics, safeguarding, and research governance bodies.
