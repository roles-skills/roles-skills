# Data protection officer

> This is an illustrative reference profile for a generic digital health care organisation. It is not an official job description for any employer, and its job evaluation scores are not a formal evaluation.

**Family:** [Information governance](../index.md#information-governance)  
**Bands:** 8a, 8b, 8c  
**UK GDaD PCF role:** none (this reference defines the role)  
**ESCO occupations:** [data protection officer](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/1619d7f3-7d95-407c-a20e-47745bfcde73) (ISCO-08 2619)

## Summary

The data protection officer (DPO) independently monitors the organisation's compliance with data protection law, advises on its duties, and is the contact point for the regulator and for people whose data it holds. In a digital health care organisation, the DPO gives expert advice on the lawful, fair, and transparent use of health data in products, analytics, research, and data sharing.

## In a digital health care organisation

- Data protection law gives the DPO a protected, independent position, reporting to the highest level of management, so the role must be free from conflicts of interest.
- Data protection law gives health data extra protection, and much of it is also subject to a duty of confidentiality, so the DPO advises on both.
- Large-scale processing of health data, data linkage, and AI often need data protection impact assessments and, sometimes, prior consultation with the regulator.
- Patients and the public expect transparency, so the DPO helps the organisation explain clearly how it uses their data.

## Band 8a: Deputy data protection officer

Supports the DPO in monitoring compliance and giving advice, leads complex casework, and deputises for the DPO.

### Responsibilities

- Advise teams on data protection law, including lawful basis, transparency, and individual rights.
- Review high-risk data protection impact assessments and draft the DPO's advice.
- Monitor compliance through audits, spot checks, and reviews of records of processing.
- Lead the investigation of serious data breaches and prepare notifications to the regulator.
- Handle complex complaints and requests from individuals about their data.
- Deputise for the DPO with senior leaders and the regulator.

## Band 8b: Data protection officer

The organisation's statutory data protection officer, independently monitoring compliance, advising senior leaders and the board, and acting as the contact point for the regulator and the public.

### Responsibilities

- Monitor the organisation's compliance with data protection law and its own policies, independently.
- Advise the board, senior leaders, and teams on their data protection duties.
- Give formal advice on high-risk data protection impact assessments, and decide when to consult the regulator.
- Act as the contact point for the regulator and for people whose data the organisation holds.
- Oversee breach notification decisions and the organisation's response to regulator enquiries.
- Report on data protection compliance and risk to the board and its committees.

## Band 8c: Senior data protection officer

The data protection officer for a large organisation or a group of organisations that share a DPO, leading the data protection function and setting its standards.

### Responsibilities

- Act as the statutory DPO for a large organisation or a group of care organisations.
- Lead the data protection function, its staff, and its work programme.
- Set the data protection framework and standards across the organisation or group.
- Give the final advice on the highest-risk processing, such as large-scale data linkage and AI.
- Lead engagement with the regulator, partners, and patient and public groups on data use.
- Report to the board on data protection risk, compliance, and public trust.

## ESCO occupations and skills

### data protection officer

- **URI:** <http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/1619d7f3-7d95-407c-a20e-47745bfcde73>
- **ESCO code:** 2619.4  ·  **ISCO-08:** 2619 Legal professionals not elsewhere classified
- **Alternative labels:** privacy officer; privacy protection officer; DPO; data compliance officer; data protection specialist; data protection advisor; data protection counsel; data protection and security officer; GDPR expert; compliance officer data privacy; data protection legal advisor; legal and data protection officer

> Data protection officers ensure that the processing of personal data in an organisation is compliant with data protection standards and with the obligations set out in the applicable legislation such as GDPR. They elaborate and implement the organisation policy related to data protection, are responsible for data protection impact assessments and handle complaints and requests from third parties and regulatory agencies. Data protection officers lead investigations into potential data breaches, conduct internal audits and act as point of contact within the organisation on any matters related to the processing of personal data. Data protection officers may develop training programmes and provide training to other employees on data protection procedures.
