# Financial accountant

> This is an illustrative reference profile for a generic digital health care organisation. It is not an official job description for any employer, and its job evaluation scores are not a formal evaluation.

**Family:** [Finance](../index.md#finance)  
**Bands:** 5, 6, 7, 8a, 8b  
**UK GDaD PCF role:** none (this reference defines the role)  
**ESCO occupations:** [accountant](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/eda0d957-3c3c-4139-b89a-a18bc9e18897) (ISCO-08 2411); [public finance accountant](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/78db356f-a96a-4456-ab29-2de62e52f830) (ISCO-08 2411)

## Summary

Financial accountants make sure the organisation's accounts are complete, accurate, and follow accounting standards and public sector reporting rules. They manage the balance sheet, prepare the annual accounts and statutory returns, manage cash and treasury, account for assets, and work with auditors. In a digital health care organisation, much of the complexity comes from accounting for software, data assets, cloud arrangements, and multi-year technology contracts.

## In a digital health care organisation

- Accounting for digital spending needs judgement, for example whether software development, configuration of cloud services, or licences can be treated as capital assets.
- The organisation must account for public money to funders and oversight bodies, often against strict capital and cash limits.
- Multi-year licence and support contracts create prepayments, commitments, and sometimes leases that must be accounted for correctly.
- Intangible assets, such as software the organisation builds, need valuing, depreciating, and reviewing for impairment as products change.
- External audit of public bodies is thorough and public, so evidence and working papers must be of a high standard.

## Band 5: Trainee financial accountant

Trainee financial accountants prepare balance sheet reconciliations, journals, and working papers for the accounts while working towards a recognised accountancy qualification.

### Responsibilities

- Prepare balance sheet reconciliations and clear reconciling items.
- Post month-end and year-end journals, such as accruals, prepayments, and depreciation.
- Maintain the fixed asset register, including software and hardware assets.
- Prepare working papers and evidence for the annual accounts and audit.
- Prepare cash flow information and bank reconciliations.
- Work towards a recognised accountancy qualification.

## Band 6: Financial accountant

Financial accountants are qualified accountants who manage the balance sheet and prepare sections of the annual accounts, statutory returns, and tax returns.

### Responsibilities

- Manage the month-end and year-end close for the balance sheet.
- Prepare sections of the annual accounts and supporting notes.
- Account for capital spending, including deciding which digital costs can be capitalised.
- Prepare sales tax returns and advise on the tax treatment of contracts.
- Answer auditors' questions and agree actions on their findings.
- Supervise and develop trainee accountants.

## Band 7: Senior financial accountant

Senior financial accountants lead the annual accounts process and the accounting for complex areas, such as intangible assets, leases, and cloud arrangements.

### Responsibilities

- Plan and lead the annual accounts timetable and the external audit.
- Write accounting papers on complex judgements, such as capitalising software development or cloud configuration costs.
- Manage capital accounting, including asset lives, depreciation, and impairment of software assets.
- Manage cash forecasting and drawdown of funding within cash limits.
- Prepare statutory and funder returns, such as consolidation and capital returns.
- Manage and develop a small team of financial accountants.

## Band 8a: Financial accounting manager

Financial accounting managers run financial accounting, treasury, and tax for the organisation, and make sure controls and audit actions are in place.

### Responsibilities

- Manage the financial accounting, treasury, and tax functions.
- Own the organisation's accounting policies and their application to new types of spending, such as cloud and data services.
- Manage bank accounts, banking arrangements, and the cash flow forecast.
- Manage tax compliance and tax risk, including on technology contracts and shared services.
- Lead the response to internal and external audit findings on financial accounting and controls.
- Line manage financial accountants and treasury staff.

## Band 8b: Head of financial accounts

Heads of financial accounts lead the organisation's statutory reporting, financial control, treasury, and tax, and are the main contact for external auditors.

### Responsibilities

- Lead the preparation of the annual report and accounts, and present them to the audit committee.
- Advise directors and the audit committee on significant accounting judgements and new standards.
- Own the financial rules, financial procedures, and scheme of delegation.
- Lead the relationship with external auditors and oversight bodies on financial reporting.
- Set treasury and tax policy, and manage financial risks.
- Lead and develop the financial accounting, treasury, and transactional teams.

### public finance accountant

- **URI:** <http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/78db356f-a96a-4456-ab29-2de62e52f830>
- **ESCO code:** 2411.1.11  ·  **ISCO-08:** 2411 Accountants
- **Alternative labels:** treasury accountant; treasury officer; treasury manager; finance accountant of government administration; public finance officer; public finance manager; finance accountant

> Public finance accountants head the treasury department of a governmental institution. They manage the institution's financial administration, expenditure and income generation, and compliance with taxation and other financial legislation. They perform administrative duties to ensure record keeping, develop plans for budget management and perform financial forecasts.
