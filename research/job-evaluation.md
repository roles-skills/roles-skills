# Job evaluation scheme

This reference uses a generic 16-factor points scheme to show why each role level sits in its band. The factors, levels, points, and band ranges are in [`data/job-evaluation.yaml`](../data/job-evaluation.yaml).

The scores in this reference are **illustrative**. A real evaluation scores the actual job, from an agreed job description, by a trained panel that includes both management and staff representatives.

## Scoring principles

- **Score the job, not the person.** The evaluation is about the work, not about how well the current post holder does it or what qualifications they hold.
- **Score the normal requirements.** Score what the job regularly needs, not rare or one-off events.
- **Score the minimum knowledge needed.** Knowledge is scored by the level needed to do the job, gained by any route: qualifications, training, or equivalent experience.
- **Best fit.** Choose the level whose summary best fits the job as a whole.
- **Effort and conditions are real but modest.** Most digital and office jobs score level 2 for working conditions, because of near-continuous screen use. Mental effort is often level 3 or 4, because of frequent or prolonged concentration on unpredictable work such as debugging, analysis, or incident response.

## Calibration: typical factor levels by band

These typical profiles were built for digital, data, and corporate roles in a digital health care organisation. Each one totals within its band. Role levels in this reference start from the profile for their band, then move factors up or down to fit the role. For example:

- A service desk role has more communication and less knowledge.
- An architect role has more analytical, research, and information-resources responsibility.
- A people manager has more responsibility for people and for finances.
- A clinical role has more responsibility for patient care.

Notes on the patterns:

- **Physical skills** are often level 3 for roles that need advanced keyboard skills, which covers most technical and data roles at Bands 3 to 7. They drop to level 2 for senior roles, where keyboard work is less central.
- **Patient and client care** is level 1 for most digital and corporate roles. It is higher only where the role directly advises on or supports patient care, for example level 4 to 6 for clinical informatics and clinical safety roles.
- **Information resources** is the factor where digital roles differ most from other roles. Designing and developing major information systems is level 5, and managing them across the organisation is level 6.
- **Emotional effort** rises where the role regularly handles distressing material, for example clinical records, serious incidents, complaints, or employee relations cases.

`scripts/build.py` adds up the points for every role level and fails if a total falls outside its band.
