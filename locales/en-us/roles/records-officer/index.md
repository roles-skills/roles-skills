# Health records officer

> This is an illustrative reference profile for a generic digital health care organization. It is not an official job description for any employer, and its job evaluation scores are not a formal evaluation.

**Family:** [Information governance](../../#information-governance)  
**Bands:** 2, 3, 4, 5  
**UK GDaD PCF role:** none (this reference defines the role)  
**ESCO occupations:** [medical records clerk](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/14eb2c85-fce5-40b6-bcd0-46ac99994b0d) (ISCO-08 3252); [medical records manager](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/983c2816-a339-4817-97f4-151a9ff3e64d) (ISCO-08 3252)

## Summary

Health records staff make sure that patient and corporate records are created, stored, tracked, retrieved, and disposed of correctly, whether they are on paper or in digital systems. They keep records available for care, protect them from loss and unauthorised access, and support the move from paper to digital records.

## In a digital health care organization

- Patient records must be available when clinicians need them, so delays or lost records can directly affect care.
- Records contain highly confidential information, so staff follow strict confidentiality and access rules and may regularly read distressing content.
- Most organizations hold both paper and digital records, so the role includes scanning, indexing, and managing hybrid records.
- Records must be kept for set periods under retention schedules and then archived or destroyed securely, with an audit trail.
- Records teams often process requests for access to health records, which have legal time limits.

## Band 2: Records assistant

Files, retrieves, tracks, scans, and prepares records, following set procedures with supervision close by.

### Responsibilities

- File, retrieve, and track paper records, and prepare them for clinics and requests.
- Scan and index records into the electronic records system, checking quality.
- Enter and update record details accurately in tracking systems.
- Keep records secure and report any lost records or breaches straight away.
- Move and store records safely, including boxes and trolleys of case notes.

## Band 3: Records officer

Handles a range of records processes, some non-routine, such as tracing missing records, preparing records for access requests, and applying retention schedules.

### Responsibilities

- Trace missing records and resolve tracking problems.
- Prepare records for access requests, checking that they are complete before review.
- Apply retention schedules and prepare records for archiving or secure destruction.
- Check scanned records for quality and fix indexing errors.
- Answer enquiries from clinicians, care staff, and patients about records.
- Show new records assistants how to follow procedures.

## Band 4: Senior records officer

Supervises a records team day to day and runs records processes such as access requests, audits, and retention reviews.

### Responsibilities

- Supervise records staff day to day, allocating work and checking quality.
- Process requests for access to health records within legal time limits, under the guidance of information governance colleagues.
- Run record-keeping and tracking audits and report the results.
- Keep records tracking and electronic records systems accurate and up to date.
- Train staff in records procedures and confidentiality.
- Investigate lost records and other incidents, and recommend improvements.

## Band 5: Health records manager

Manages the health records service, its staff, systems, and procedures, and leads improvement, including the move from paper to digital records.

### Responsibilities

- Manage the health records service, its staff, and its performance.
- Write and maintain records procedures, in line with records management policy and standards.
- Manage records tracking and electronic records systems for several services.
- Lead scanning and digitization projects and the move to digital records.
- Run the retention and disposal program and records audits.
- Advise clinicians, managers, and information governance colleagues on records management.
