# Data protection officer

> This is an illustrative reference profile for a generic digital health care organization. It is not an official job description for any employer, and its job evaluation scores are not a formal evaluation.

**Family:** [Information governance](../../#information-governance)  
**Bands:** 8a, 8b, 8c  
**UK GDaD PCF role:** none (this reference defines the role)  
**ESCO occupations:** [data protection officer](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/1619d7f3-7d95-407c-a20e-47745bfcde73) (ISCO-08 2619)

## Summary

The data protection officer (DPO) independently monitors the organization's compliance with data protection law, advises on its duties, and is the contact point for the regulator and for people whose data it holds. In a digital health care organization, the DPO gives expert advice on the lawful, fair, and transparent use of health data in products, analytics, research, and data sharing.

## In a digital health care organization

- Data protection law gives the DPO a protected, independent position, reporting to the highest level of management, so the role must be free from conflicts of interest.
- Data protection law gives health data extra protection, and much of it is also subject to a duty of confidentiality, so the DPO advises on both.
- Large-scale processing of health data, data linkage, and AI often need data protection impact assessments and, sometimes, prior consultation with the regulator.
- Patients and the public expect transparency, so the DPO helps the organization explain clearly how it uses their data.

## Band 8a: Deputy data protection officer

Supports the DPO in monitoring compliance and giving advice, leads complex casework, and deputizes for the DPO.

### Responsibilities

- Advise teams on data protection law, including lawful basis, transparency, and individual rights.
- Review high-risk data protection impact assessments and draft the DPO's advice.
- Monitor compliance through audits, spot checks, and reviews of records of processing.
- Lead the investigation of serious data breaches and prepare notifications to the regulator.
- Handle complex complaints and requests from individuals about their data.
- Deputize for the DPO with senior leaders and the regulator.

## Band 8b: Data protection officer

The organization's statutory data protection officer, independently monitoring compliance, advising senior leaders and the board, and acting as the contact point for the regulator and the public.

### Responsibilities

- Monitor the organization's compliance with data protection law and its own policies, independently.
- Advise the board, senior leaders, and teams on their data protection duties.
- Give formal advice on high-risk data protection impact assessments, and decide when to consult the regulator.
- Act as the contact point for the regulator and for people whose data the organization holds.
- Oversee breach notification decisions and the organization's response to regulator enquiries.
- Report on data protection compliance and risk to the board and its committees.

## Band 8c: Senior data protection officer

The data protection officer for a large organization or a group of organizations that share a DPO, leading the data protection function and setting its standards.

### Responsibilities

- Act as the statutory DPO for a large organization or a group of care organizations.
- Lead the data protection function, its staff, and its work programme.
- Set the data protection framework and standards across the organization or group.
- Give the final advice on the highest-risk processing, such as large-scale data linkage and AI.
- Lead engagement with the regulator, partners, and patient and public groups on data use.
- Report to the board on data protection risk, compliance, and public trust.
