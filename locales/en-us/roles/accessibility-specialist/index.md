# Accessibility specialist

> This is an illustrative reference profile for a generic digital health care organization. It is not an official job description for any employer, and its job evaluation scores are not a formal evaluation.

**Family:** [User-centred design](../../#user-centred-design)  
**Bands:** 5, 6, 7, 8b  
**UK GDaD PCF role:** [Accessibility specialist](https://understand-digital-data-roles-skills.service.gov.uk/role/accessibility-specialist/)  
**ESCO occupations:** [ICT accessibility tester](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/50af07f0-7a75-424e-a66d-5a9deea10f4c) (ISCO-08 2519)

## Summary

Accessibility specialists help the organization make its digital health services usable by as many people as possible, including disabled people and people with long-term conditions. They test services against accessibility standards and with assistive technologies, advise and train teams, and help embed accessibility into how services, clinical systems, and health information are designed, bought, and built.

## In a digital health care organization

- Many people who need health and care services most are disabled, older, or living with long-term conditions, so inaccessible services can stop people getting care.
- Clinicians and care staff also have access needs, so internal clinical systems, including those bought from suppliers, must be accessible too.
- Health information is often complex and published in many formats, such as documents, letters, and video, so specialists help teams make all of it accessible, including easy read and alternative formats.
- Accessibility problems in clinical workflows can become safety problems, for example when a warning is not announced by a screen reader, so specialists work with clinical safety officers on these risks.

## UK GDaD PCF role description (English original)

> An accessibility specialist provides support, advice and guidance to other roles in the Government Digital and Data profession about how to create accessible digital services.
> 
> You will use knowledge of accessibility guidelines, provide technical expertise and bring the voice of disabled users to ensure that product teams make digital services that can be used by everyone.

## Band 5: Junior accessibility specialist

**UK GDaD PCF level: Junior accessibility specialist**

> A junior accessibility specialist learns on the job by assisting with accessibility testing.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - develop skills while working under supervision to provide accessibility testing
> - understand and use different types of testing
> - be aware of but not responsible for assistive technology testing
> - engage with and contribute to the cross-government accessibility community

### Responsibilities

- Test web pages, apps, and documents against accessibility standards, under supervision.
- Record and explain accessibility issues clearly so teams can fix them.
- Help with testing using assistive technologies, such as screen readers and magnifiers.
- Help keep accessibility statements and issue logs up to date.

## Band 6: Accessibility specialist

**UK GDaD PCF level: Accessibility specialist**

> An accessibility specialist can support teams in creating accessible services.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - coach and mentor more junior colleagues
> - support in providing training materials and learning content
> - perform accessibility audits
> - engage with and contribute to the cross-government accessibility community

### Responsibilities

- Carry out accessibility audits of patient-facing services, clinical systems, and health information.
- Test with a range of assistive technologies and support research with disabled users.
- Advise teams on fixes and on accessible patterns for forms, alerts, and safety-critical information.
- Review supplier products for accessibility during procurement and acceptance.
- Create training and guidance, and coach more junior colleagues.

## Band 7: Senior accessibility specialist

**UK GDaD PCF level: Senior accessibility specialist**

> A senior accessibility specialist works with people at a range of role levels to embed accessibility into the output of teams.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - coach and mentor more junior colleagues
> - input into accessibility strategy with business areas or teams
> - create and deliver accessibility training
> - engage with teams across different levels and capabilities
> - potentially manage or lead individuals or sub-teams
> - engage with and contribute to the cross-government accessibility community

### Responsibilities

- Embed accessibility into teams' ways of working, from discovery to live service.
- Contribute to accessibility strategy, including how accessibility is assured in procurement and service assessments.
- Work with clinical safety officers where accessibility issues could affect patient safety.
- Create and deliver accessibility training for designers, developers, content designers, and suppliers.
- Lead or manage other accessibility specialists.

## Band 8b: Head of accessibility

**UK GDaD PCF level: Head of accessibility**

> A head of accessibility is an expert practitioner who can create an accessibility vision or strategy for an organization.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - define and assure best practice while influencing, leading and mentoring others
> - influence and develop organisational strategy and priorities, working with counterpart colleagues across government
> - ensure the right conditions and environment for accessibility specialists to work effectively
> - develop departmental capability by supporting internal movement of staff between roles, shaping career paths and recruiting talent
> - understand and own the overall approach to accessibility risk (including how accessibility risk is communicated and how corporate legal compliance risk is managed within the organization)
> - engage with and contribute to the cross-government accessibility community

### Responsibilities

- Set the organization's accessibility vision, strategy, and standards, for patient-facing and internal services.
- Assure compliance with accessibility law and standards across services, and report on it to senior leaders.
- Make sure accessibility requirements are part of procurement and supplier contracts for clinical systems.
- Influence organisational priorities so that disabled patients and staff are not excluded.
- Lead and develop the accessibility team and community of practice.
