# Content designer

> This is an illustrative reference profile for a generic digital health care organization. It is not an official job description for any employer, and its job evaluation scores are not a formal evaluation.

**Family:** [User-centered design](../../#user-centered-design)  
**Bands:** 4, 5, 6, 7, 8b, 8c  
**UK GDaD PCF role:** [Content designer](https://understand-digital-data-roles-skills.service.gov.uk/role/content-designer/)  
**ESCO occupations:** [web content manager](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/a74f6c51-37e5-423e-aa5b-044e98663027) (ISCO-08 2513); [technical communicator](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/8cb59bc4-1f39-477e-beea-effed12d186c) (ISCO-08 2641)

## Summary

Content designers make health and care information and services clear, so that patients, carers, clinicians, and care staff can understand what they need to know and do. They design content from evidence of user needs, write in plain English, work with clinical experts to keep it accurate, and look after content across its whole life, from creation to review and retirement.

## In a digital health care organization

- People often read health content when they are worried, unwell, or in a hurry, so content must be easy to understand and act on for people with low health literacy.
- Wrong or out-of-date health information can cause harm, so content designers follow a clinical assurance process with review dates and named clinical reviewers.
- Some topics are sensitive or high risk, such as urgent symptoms, mental health, medicines, and end of life care, so content designers work closely with clinicians and user researchers on tone and accuracy.
- Clinical systems also need clear content, such as labels, instructions, and alerts, so content designers help reduce errors in clinical workflows.
- Health content must be accessible and inclusive, including for people who use assistive technology, read in another language, or need easy read formats.

## UK GDaD PCF role description (English original)

> Content designers make things easier for people to understand and use. This can involve working on a single piece of content or on the end-to-end journey of a service to help users complete their goal and government deliver a policy intent. In this role your work may involve the creation of, or change to, a transaction, product or single piece of content that stretches across digital and offline channels.
> 
> In this role, you will:
> - make sure appropriate content is shown to a user in the right place and in the best format
> - start from discovery and work closely with user researchers, service designers and interaction designers

## Band 4: Associate content designer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Associate content designer**

> As a trainee in an entry-level position, working under supervision, you need aptitude, potential and an understanding of the role. You will perform basic standard checks and copy edits.

### Responsibilities

- Learn content design methods, the style guide, and the clinical review process through training and supervised work.
- Carry out standard checks, copy edits, and updates to existing content.
- Publish content using the content management system, following assurance steps.
- Check content for accessibility, such as headings, link text, and alternative text.

## Band 6: Content designer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Content designer**

> A content designer is responsible for creating, evaluating, updating and reviewing content at all stages of the end-to-end user journey.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - be comfortable using evidence, data and research to make content decisions
> - build relationships across government to focus on the needs of the user and to influence stakeholders
> - contribute to and use the style guides and content patterns
> - engage with and contribute to the cross-government content community

### Responsibilities

- Design content across whole user journeys, such as finding a service, preparing for an appointment, and aftercare.
- Design content for clinical systems, such as labels, instructions, and alerts, to reduce the chance of error.
- Agree accuracy with clinical experts while keeping content clear and usable.
- Test content with users, including people with low health literacy and people who use assistive technology.
- Contribute to the style guide and content patterns.

## Band 8b: Lead content designer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Lead content designer**

> A lead content designer is an expert practitioner and leader who directs a team of content designers. They assure the content quality across whole teams and make sure content aligns to strategy and objectives while meeting the needs of users.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - work closely with service managers, programme directors and senior stakeholders to resource teams, resolve problems and develop future projects
> - promote the content design discipline, engage with the cross-government community and keep up to date with industry changes

### Responsibilities

- Direct a team of content designers and assure content quality across several teams.
- Make sure health content meets clinical assurance, accessibility, and style standards.
- Work with senior clinical, communications, and product leaders to resource teams and resolve content problems.
- Lead content for the organization's most sensitive or high-risk information.
- Line manage and develop content designers.

## Band 8c: Head of content design

**UK GDaD PCF level: Head of content design**

> A head of content design is an expert practitioner with broad industry experience who can define and assure best practice while influencing, leading and mentoring others.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - develop a strategy for content that meets the organisation’s objectives
> - lead a team capable of executing that strategy
> - work with senior stakeholders to influence organisational strategy
> - prioritise and collaborate with counterpart colleagues across government
> - champion good content design practice within government and industry

### Responsibilities

- Develop a content strategy that meets the organization's objectives and the needs of patients and staff.
- Set the organization's standards for health information content and its clinical assurance.
- Lead a content design team able to carry out the strategy, including recruitment and development.
- Work with senior clinical and communications leaders to manage health information risks.
- Prioritize content work across the organization.
