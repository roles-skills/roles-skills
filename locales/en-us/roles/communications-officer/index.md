# Communications officer

> This is an illustrative reference profile for a generic digital health care organization. It is not an official job description for any employer, and its job evaluation scores are not a formal evaluation.

**Family:** [Corporate services](../../#corporate-services)  
**Bands:** 4, 5, 6, 7, 8b  
**UK GDaD PCF role:** none (this reference defines the role)  
**ESCO occupations:** [public relations officer](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/c0489e22-6a1b-4e52-974a-eb71bbb42134) (ISCO-08 2432); [communication manager](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/cd7b838c-51f6-41ec-9067-bb974c0f991b) (ISCO-08 1222)

## Summary

Communications staff help the organization explain what it does to staff, patients, the public, partners, and the media. They write and publish content, run internal communications and campaigns, manage digital and social media channels, handle media enquiries, and protect the organization's reputation, especially during incidents and outages.

## In a digital health care organization

- Communications about health services must be accurate, clear, and accessible, because patients may act on them.
- Campaigns often aim to change behavior, such as encouraging patients and care staff to use a new digital service.
- Service outages, cyber incidents, and data breaches need fast, careful communications with patients, staff, partners, and the media.
- Stories about patients need consent and must protect confidentiality.

## Band 4: Communications officer

Writes and publishes content for internal and external channels and supports the communications team with routine media and campaign work.

### Responsibilities

- Write and edit news, intranet content, newsletters, and social media posts.
- Publish approved content on the website, intranet, and social media.
- Log media enquiries and monitor coverage.
- Support campaigns and events with content and logistics.
- Check content for accuracy, accessibility, and plain English.

- Experience of writing for different audiences, or a qualification in communications or a related subject, or equivalent experience.

## Band 5: Senior communications officer

Plans and delivers communications for projects and services, handles routine media enquiries, and manages channels.

### Responsibilities

- Plan and deliver communications for projects and service changes.
- Respond to routine media enquiries and draft press releases and statements.
- Manage content plans for digital and social media channels, and report on performance.
- Write case studies, including patient stories, with consent.
- Advise colleagues on writing clearly for patients and the public.
- Coach communications officers.

## Band 6: Communications lead

Leads communications for a major program or area, such as internal communications or digital channels, and handles sensitive media work.

### Responsibilities

- Lead communications and engagement for a major program, such as a new clinical system.
- Plan and evaluate campaigns that encourage patients and staff to adopt digital services.
- Handle sensitive media enquiries and prepare spokespeople.
- Plan communications for incidents and service outages with operational teams.
- Advise leaders on how to communicate change and difficult messages.
- Supervise communications officers.

## Band 7: Communications manager

Manages the communications team's work and channels, and leads media handling and communications for incidents.

### Responsibilities

- Manage the communications team, its work plan, and its budget.
- Lead reactive and proactive media work, including during incidents and data breaches.
- Manage the organization's channels, brand, and content standards.
- Advise directors on communications and reputational risks.
- Evaluate communications and report on their effect.
- Work with partner organizations on joint communications.

## Band 8b: Head of communications

Leads communications and engagement for the organization, sets its strategy, and advises the board on reputation.

### Responsibilities

- Set the communications and engagement strategy for the organization.
- Lead and develop the communications function and manage its budget.
- Advise the chief executive and board on reputation, stakeholder engagement, and communications risk.
- Lead crisis communications during major incidents.
- Build relationships with senior partners, journalists, and patient groups.
- Shape how the organization explains its digital services and its use of data to the public.
