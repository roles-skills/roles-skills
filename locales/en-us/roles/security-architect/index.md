**Family:** [Architecture](../../#architecture)

**ESCO occupations:** [ICT system architect](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/e1c72b5f-4c5c-487c-a6df-e84b64a51dae) (ISCO-08 2511); [cybersecurity risk manager](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/7754d570-9519-48c2-b1c9-8e165f8bca0f) (ISCO-08 2529)

## Summary

Security architects design the security of the organization's digital health services, so that patient information stays confidential, accurate, and available when care needs it. They set security patterns and controls, assess the security risk of new designs and suppliers, and help teams make risk-based decisions that keep services both safe and usable for clinicians and patients.

## In a digital health care organization

- Health organizations are frequent targets of ransomware and data theft, and an attack can stop clinical services, so availability is as important as confidentiality.
- Security controls must not get in the way of urgent care, so designs balance strong authentication with fast access in clinical settings.
- Connected medical devices and legacy clinical systems often cannot be patched quickly, so architects design compensating controls such as segmentation.
- Many staff, partner organizations, and suppliers need access to patient records, so identity, role-based access, and audit are central.
- Security designs must support information governance duties and published security standards, such as ISO/IEC 27001.

## Band 8a: Security architect

**UK GDaD PCF level: Security architect**

> A security architect creates and designs security for a system or service, maintains security documentation and develops architecture patterns and security approaches to new technologies.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - recommend security controls and identify solutions that support a business objective
> - provide specialist advice and recommend approaches across teams and various stakeholders
> - communicate widely with other stakeholders
> - advise on important security-related technologies and assess the risk associated with proposed changes
> - inspire and influence others to execute security principles
> - help review other people’s work

### Responsibilities

- Design security controls for services and systems, including identity, encryption, logging, and network protection.
- Assess the security of new designs and supplier products, and record risks and recommended controls.
- Develop security patterns that delivery teams can reuse, such as for patient login and staff single sign-on.
- Advise on compensating controls for medical devices and legacy clinical systems that cannot be updated easily.
- Work with information governance and clinical safety colleagues so that security, privacy, and safety risks are assessed together.
- Coach security engineers and other architects in secure design.

## Band 8b: Lead security architect

**UK GDaD PCF level: Lead security architect**

> A lead security architect undertakes complex work of a high risk level, often working on several projects.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - interact with senior stakeholders across departments
> - reach and influence a wide range of people across larger teams and communities
> - research and apply innovative security architecture solutions to new or existing problems and be able to justify and communicate design decisions
> - develop vision, principles and strategy for security architects for one project or technology
> - work out subtle security needs
> - understand the impact of decisions, balancing requirements and deciding between approaches
> - produce particular patterns and support quality assurance
> - be the point of escalation for architects in lower grade roles
> - lead the technical design of systems and services

### Responsibilities

- Lead security architecture for several high-risk projects or a major area, such as clinical systems or cloud hosting.
- Set security principles and standards for designs in your area and assure them at design authorities.
- Advise senior risk owners on security risks and the options to manage them.
- Design security for data shared with partner organizations and suppliers.
- Lead and develop security architects.

## Band 8c: Principal security architect

**UK GDaD PCF level: Principal security architect**

> A principal security architect works on services of high complexity and risk, making decisions to enable the business to achieve its needs.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - work on projects with high strategic impact, setting a strategy that can be used in the long term and across the breadth of the organisation
> - communicate with a broad range of senior stakeholders and be responsible for defining the vision, principles and strategy for security architects
> - recommend security design across several projects or technologies, up to an organisational or inter-organisational level
> - have a deep and evolving level of technical expertise, so you can act as an exemplar
> - make and influence important business and architectural decisions
> - research, identify, validate and adopt new technologies and methodologies
> - be a recognised expert and demonstrate this expertise by solving unprecedented issues and problems
> - further the profession, demonstrating and sharing best practice within and outside the organisation

### Responsibilities

- Set the organization's security architecture vision, principles, and strategy.
- Advise the executive team and board on cyber security risk to patient care and services.
- Make sure cyber resilience, including recovery from a major attack, is designed into critical clinical services.
- Represent the organization in cross-organization cyber security and standards communities.
- Lead the security architecture practice and develop its people.
