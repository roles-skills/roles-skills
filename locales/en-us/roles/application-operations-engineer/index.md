# Application operations engineer

> This is an illustrative reference profile for a generic digital health care organization. It is not an official job description for any employer, and its job evaluation scores are not a formal evaluation.

**Family:** [IT operations](../../#it-operations)  
**Bands:** 4, 5, 6, 7, 8b  
**UK GDaD PCF role:** [Application operations engineer](https://understand-digital-data-roles-skills.service.gov.uk/role/application-operations-engineer/)  
**ESCO occupations:** [ICT system administrator](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/9e2e6e1e-363b-4e1b-a673-7bc0f7343300) (ISCO-08 2522); [database administrator](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/8c57af09-719c-42b3-be40-6ed4946236cc) (ISCO-08 2521)

## Summary

Application operations engineers keep the organization's applications running in live use, including clinical systems such as the electronic patient record and prescribing, laboratory, and imaging systems. They monitor applications, run scheduled jobs, manage users and access, deploy releases and fixes, and work with suppliers to resolve faults. Their work lets clinical and corporate staff rely on these systems at every hour of the day.

## In a digital health care organization

- Clinical applications run 24 hours a day, so engineers take part in on-call rotas and plan maintenance for the quietest clinical periods.
- Failed scheduled jobs and interfaces can delay test results, referrals, or discharge letters, so engineers watch them closely and escalate quickly.
- Planned and unplanned downtime needs business continuity arrangements, such as read-only copies and downtime reports, so that clinicians can keep treating patients.
- Many clinical applications are supplied and hosted by third parties, so engineers manage suppliers and their changes carefully.
- Changes to clinical applications follow the clinical risk management process, and some applications may be regulated as medical devices.

## Band 4: Associate application operations engineer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Associate application operations engineer**

> As a trainee, you will work in an established team and support application operations engineers in their daily work, under their direction.

### Responsibilities

- Monitor applications, interfaces, and scheduled jobs, and report failures to experienced engineers.
- Create and change user accounts and access in applications, following role-based access rules.
- Carry out routine checks and maintenance tasks using runbooks.
- Record incidents and changes accurately in the service management tool.
- Work towards an IT apprenticeship or equivalent qualification.

## Band 5: Application operations engineer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Application operations engineer**

> An application operations engineer maintains services and is responsible for conducting user and security administration.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - monitor applications and be accountable for batch processes
> - in some organizations, carry out the duties of an end user computing engineer, such as fixing technical problems, maintaining hardware and liaising with the problem or incident manager

### Responsibilities

- Monitor clinical and corporate applications and their interfaces, and act on alerts and failed jobs before they affect patient care.
- Run and check scheduled jobs, such as overnight data loads and interface queues, and resolve or escalate failures.
- Manage application users, roles, and access, including removing access for leavers and supporting access reviews.
- Produce and test downtime reports and other business continuity outputs for clinical applications.
- Log faults with suppliers and track them to resolution.
- Take part in the on-call rota, with guidance, for out-of-hours application faults.

## Band 6: Senior application operations engineer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Senior application operations engineer**

> Dealing with more complex issues, a senior application operations engineer is responsible for code deployment and co-ordinating change-based activities, such as taking services live. Depending on the complexity and size of a project and the likely impact it will have on the business, you might manage and maintain certain aspects of an application.

### Responsibilities

- Deploy releases, patches, and configuration changes to live applications, following change and clinical safety procedures.
- Coordinate the technical work to take new applications and major upgrades live, including cutover and rollback plans.
- Investigate complex application faults with suppliers, infrastructure teams, and clinical system specialists.
- Plan maintenance windows with clinical services, and make sure downtime procedures are ready.
- Provide evidence for clinical safety cases, such as test and deployment records.
- Act as an escalation point on the on-call rota for out-of-hours faults.

## Band 7: Lead application operations engineer

### Responsibilities

- Set standards for user and security administration across clinical applications, working with the security team.
- Report on application performance and availability to IT operations leads, and agree the changes needed.
- Lead the technical response to major incidents affecting clinical applications.
- Make sure business continuity and disaster recovery arrangements for clinical applications are tested.
- Lead and develop application operations engineers.

## Band 8b: Principal application operations engineer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Principal application operations engineer**

> A principal application operations engineer owns the application strategy and leads on resourcing, learning and development for their team.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - have expert technical understanding
> - be accountable for vendor and supplier management
> - potentially manage and maintain certain aspects of an application (depending on the complexity and size of a project, and the likely impact it will have on the business)

### Responsibilities

- Own the application operations strategy across the organization's clinical and corporate applications.
- Manage strategic suppliers of clinical applications, including service levels, contract performance, and supplier changes.
- Advise senior leaders on application risks, such as unsupported software, single points of failure, and resilience against ransomware.
- Make sure the organization can keep caring for patients safely during prolonged application downtime.
- Lead resourcing, learning, and development for application operations teams.

### Typical qualifications and experience

### Band outline

- **Knowledge:** Expert knowledge across several disciplines or a large service.
- **Autonomy:** Shapes policy and strategy for a large area.
- **Scope:** Several services or teams, or a principal-level discipline.
- **Leadership:** Manages managers, or is the principal authority in a discipline.
- **Accountability:** Several services, their staff, and their budgets.
