# Network architect

> This is an illustrative reference profile for a generic digital health care organization. It is not an official job description for any employer, and its job evaluation scores are not a formal evaluation.

**Family:** [Architecture](../../#architecture)  
**Bands:** 6, 7, 8a  
**UK GDaD PCF role:** [Network architect](https://understand-digital-data-roles-skills.service.gov.uk/role/network-architect/)  
**ESCO occupations:** [ICT network architect](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/e0b544dd-b621-4126-a55e-054af25c6ea0) (ISCO-08 2523)

## Summary

Network architects design the local, wide area, wireless, and cloud networks that connect the organization's sites, staff, clinical systems, and medical devices. They make sure networks are fast, resilient, and secure enough for care that never stops, and they plan how networks change as services move to the cloud and connect with partner organizations.

## In a digital health care organization

- Clinical systems, telephony, and medical devices depend on the network, so an outage can stop care; designs need resilience and tested failover.
- Networks often carry connected medical devices that are hard to patch, so architects design segmentation and monitoring to protect them.
- Staff work across hospitals, clinics, community settings, and patients' homes, so designs must support secure mobile and remote access.
- Networks connect to shared health and care networks and partner organizations, which set their own connection and security requirements.
- Changes to clinical areas need careful planning around patient care, for example scheduling work outside busy periods.

## Band 6: Associate network architect

**UK GDaD PCF level: Associate network architect**

> An associate network architect works with network architects on designs and specifications, with guidance.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - be aware and knowledgeable of new technology solutions and produce network design policy
> - draft recommendations for network support, creating and maintaining network plans and planning infrastructure runs
> - ensure designs meet SLAs (service level agreements) and develop an understanding of common tools
> - have an understanding of one or two of the following network technologies: LAN (local area network), WAN (wide area network), wifi, data center LAN, SD (software-defined) networks, telephony, mobile solutions infrastructure, unified comms, network management solutions, network specific security and remote access technologies and approaches
> - collaborate with engineers to ensure systems are practically designed

### Responsibilities

- Draft network designs, diagrams, and specifications with guidance from network architects.
- Survey sites and gather requirements for clinical areas, including wireless coverage and device connections.
- Keep records of network topology, addressing, and connected devices up to date.
- Check that designs meet service levels and security standards before review.

## Band 7: Network architect

**UK GDaD PCF level: Network architect**

> A network architect has responsibility for network designs and specifications to support business strategies using common tools.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - research new technologies and solutions, and ensure appropriate levels of assurance
> - plan, direct and co-ordinate activities, engaging with other stakeholders to manage and implement a programme
> - agree SLAs (service level agreements) and engage with third party vendors
> - have an understanding of most of the following network technologies: LAN (local area network), WAN (wide area network), wifi, data-center LAN, SD (software-defined) networks, telephony, mobile solutions infrastructure, unified comms, network management solutions, network-specific security and remote access technologies and approaches
> - coach and mentor associate network architects where appropriate
> - collaborate with engineers to ensure systems are practically designed

### Responsibilities

- Design local, wide area, wireless, and cloud network solutions for sites and services.
- Design network segmentation for medical devices, clinical systems, and guest access.
- Plan network changes in clinical areas with service leads so that care is not disrupted.
- Agree service levels with suppliers and connection requirements with partner networks.
- Assess new network technologies and their fit with the organization's security standards.

## Band 8a: Lead network architect

**UK GDaD PCF level: Lead network architect**

> A lead network architect is accountable for designs and networks representing their business area.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - be a point of escalation
> - actively input into governance forums that determine IT strategy
> - cascade messages, standards and best practice from other architecture teams and design authorities to the network architecture team
> - act as a subject matter expert for network architects
> - represent network architects internally and externally
> - have an understanding of all network technology including LAN (local area network), WAN (wide area network), wifi, data center LAN, SD (software-defined) networks, telephony, mobile solutions infrastructure, unified comms, network management solutions, and network specific security and remote access technologies and approaches
> - champion new technologies and solutions
> - coach and mentor network architects and associate network architects
> - be a strategic thinker who can communicate network concepts to technical and non-technical stakeholders

### Responsibilities

- Own the network architecture for the organization, including its sites, data centers, and cloud connections.
- Set network design standards and assure designs at the design authority.
- Design network resilience and recovery for critical clinical services, and make sure it is tested.
- Advise on network investment, supplier contracts, and the network risks of new services.
- Act as the expert point of escalation and guide other network architects and engineers.
