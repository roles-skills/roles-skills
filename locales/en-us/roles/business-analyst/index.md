# Business analyst

> This is an illustrative reference profile for a generic digital health care organization. It is not an official job description for any employer, and its job evaluation scores are not a formal evaluation.

**Family:** [Product and delivery](../../#product-and-delivery)  
**Bands:** 4, 5, 6, 7, 8a, 8c  
**UK GDaD PCF role:** [Business analyst](https://understand-digital-data-roles-skills.service.gov.uk/role/business-analyst/)  
**ESCO occupations:** [business analyst](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/60082a99-d8ef-4e84-9290-78902681b6ed) (ISCO-08 2421); [ICT business analyst](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/fe0fa514-b48d-4a53-9757-a283c0baacf0) (ISCO-08 2511)

## Summary

Business analysts help the organization understand problems in health and care services and find the right changes to solve them. They analyze how clinical, administrative, and patient-facing processes work today, define user and business needs and requirements, and help teams make sure new digital services fit real care pathways and deliver measurable benefits.

## In a digital health care organization

- Processes cross organisational and professional boundaries, so analysts map whole care pathways, including handoffs between clinicians, care staff, patients, and other organizations.
- Requirements often carry clinical risk, so analysts work with clinical safety officers to make sure safety-related requirements are captured, traced, and tested.
- Many requirements concern sharing health data between systems, so analysts need a working grasp of interoperability standards such as HL7 FHIR and clinical terminologies such as SNOMED CT.
- Benefits in care are often about safety, quality, staff time, and patient experience, not only cost, so analysts help define measures that clinicians recognize.

## UK GDaD PCF role description (English original)

> Business analysts help teams to:
> - analyze and understand a business problem or opportunity
> - undertake research and analysis to understand how a business or business area works, considering the people, organization, processes, information, data and technology
> - identify areas for improvement, explore feasible options, analyze the effects of change and define success measures
> - identify and elaborate user and business needs to enable effective design, development and testing of services and business change
> - make decisions related to prioritization and minimum viable product by using analysis led insights
> - ensure new products and services meet business and user needs, and are aligned with organisational goals
> - understand any business and policy constraints that need to be considered, and assess the implications

## Band 4: Trainee business analyst

**UK GDaD PCF level: Trainee business analyst**

> A trainee business analyst receives direction from more senior business analysts. At this level, you will work with others or under supervision to build and develop your skills.

### Responsibilities

- Learn business analysis techniques through structured training, shadowing, and supervised work.
- Record notes and draft process maps from workshops with clinical and administrative staff.
- Maintain requirements logs, user stories, and traceability records under direction.
- Help prepare test scenarios for user acceptance testing.
- Follow information governance rules when handling documents that include patient information.

## Band 5: Junior business analyst

**UK GDaD PCF level: Junior business analyst**

> A junior business analyst receives direction from more senior business analysts but is responsible for the output of specific tasks. At this level, you will have limited skills and will work with others or under supervision. You will support stakeholder relationship management.

### Responsibilities

- Map current and future processes for clinical and administrative workflows, such as referrals, appointments, or results handling.
- Elicit and write requirements and user stories for well-defined pieces of work.
- Gather data on process performance, such as waiting times or manual workarounds, to support analysis.
- Prepare and run user acceptance test scenarios with clinical and operational users.
- Flag requirements that could affect patient safety to the team and the clinical safety officer.

## Band 6: Business analyst

**UK GDaD PCF level: Business analyst**

> A business analyst leads small to medium size projects and supports larger and more complex projects.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - manage stakeholder relationships
> - work independently
> - have a good understanding of your work area
> - develop the business analysis community by sharing best practice and mentoring others

### Responsibilities

- Lead the analysis for small and medium projects, from problem definition to benefits measurement.
- Run workshops with clinicians, care staff, and managers to understand pain points and agree options.
- Model care pathways and information flows, including data exchanged with other health and care systems.
- Define safety-related and data requirements, and trace them through design, build, and test.
- Build benefits cases with measures such as staff time released, errors avoided, or patient experience.
- Mentor junior and trainee business analysts.

## Band 7: Senior business analyst

**UK GDaD PCF level: Senior business analyst**

> A senior business analyst has a good understanding of strategic arenas and leads large or complex projects.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - have functional or people management responsibilities, or a combination of both
> - mentor others
> - develop best practice
> - own stakeholder relationships
> - manage community of practice activities within a business area

### Responsibilities

- Lead business analysis on large or complex programs, such as replacing a clinical system or redesigning a care pathway.
- Analyze options and their effect on care, safety, staff workload, and cost, and present recommendations to boards.
- Make sure clinical risk, information governance, and equality impacts are analyzed and addressed in requirements.
- Define and track the benefits of change, and set up how they will be measured after go-live.
- Line manage or lead business analysts and run community of practice activities.

## Band 8a: Lead business analyst

**UK GDaD PCF level: Lead business analyst**

> A lead business analyst has a good understanding of the enterprise arena and works in highly complex problem spaces, typically spanning multiple products or services.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - be a leader in the business analysis community (across government and externally)
> - have functional management or people management accountabilities, or a combination of both
> - actively share knowledge and be seen as a go-to person in the field
> - mentor others
> - own the main stakeholder relationships

### Responsibilities

- Lead analysis for highly complex problems that span several products, services, or organizations in a care system.
- Shape the business case and benefits realization plan for major investments.
- Set business analysis standards, such as requirements traceability for safety-related and regulated software.
- Work with clinical leads, architects, and product managers to align analysis with organisational strategy.
- Line manage business analysts and lead their professional development.

## Band 8c: Head of business analysis
