# Service designer

> This is an illustrative reference profile for a generic digital health care organization. It is not an official job description for any employer, and its job evaluation scores are not a formal evaluation.

**Family:** [User-centered design](../../#user-centered-design)  
**Bands:** 4, 5, 6, 7, 8b, 8c  
**UK GDaD PCF role:** [Service designer](https://understand-digital-data-roles-skills.service.gov.uk/role/service-designer/)  
**ESCO occupations:** [user experience analyst](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/faed411a-f920-4100-86a8-b877928b429c) (ISCO-08 2511); [user interface designer](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/96e20037-0a25-4bf6-a25e-808d1605d890) (ISCO-08 2513)

## Summary

Service designers design health and care services end to end, from the moment someone first needs help to the end of their care, across digital, phone, paper, and face-to-face channels. They work with patients, carers, clinicians, and care staff to map how services really work, find where they fail people, and design services that are safe, simple, and fair, and that work across organizational boundaries.

## In a digital health care organization

- Care pathways cross many organizations and professions, so service designers map whole journeys, including handoffs, waits, and the work patients and carers do to hold care together.
- Changes to how a service works can create new clinical risks, so service designers work with clinical safety officers to identify hazards in new service models before they go live.
- Digital services must not shut out people who cannot or will not use them, so service designers design assisted and non-digital routes alongside digital ones.
- Clinical and operational staff are part of the service, so designs must fit their workflows, workload, and professional responsibilities, not only the patient's journey.

## UK GDaD PCF role description (English original)

> Service designers design the end-to-end journey of a service. This helps a user complete their goal and government deliver a policy intent. In this role, your work may involve the creation of, or change to, transactions, products and content across both digital and offline channels provided by different parts of government.

## Band 4: Associate service designer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Associate service designer**

> As a trainee in an entry-level position, working under supervision, you will need design aptitude, potential and an understanding of the role.

### Responsibilities

- Learn service design methods through training and by working alongside experienced designers.
- Help map user journeys and service blueprints from research with patients and staff.
- Help prepare and run co-design workshops, following consent and information governance procedures.
- Document design decisions and keep design artifacts up to date.

## Band 5: Junior service designer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Junior service designer**

> Junior designers are graduates with a degree in a relevant subject or relevant work experience.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - explain design decisions, work collaboratively and have responsibility for a service
> - work independently after being given direction by more senior designers
> - independently identify user issues and important needs

### Responsibilities

- Map current journeys and service blueprints for parts of a care pathway, such as booking or follow-up.
- Design and test improvements to parts of a service, with direction from a more senior designer.
- Run co-design sessions with patients, carers, and staff, adapting them so everyone can take part.
- Identify where a service creates extra work or risk for patients or staff, and raise it with the team.

## Band 6: Service designer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Service designer**

> A service designer is a confident and competent designer who can develop designs based on evidence of user needs and organisational outcomes.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - be trusted to make good decisions
> - recognise when to ask for further guidance and support
> - contribute to the development of design concepts
> - interpret evidence-based research and incorporate this into your work

### Responsibilities

- Design end-to-end services across channels, including assisted and non-digital routes for people who need them.
- Map care pathways across organizations and design better handoffs between teams and services.
- Design how the service works for staff, including roles, workload, and workflows, with operational leads.
- Work with clinical safety officers to identify hazards in new service models and design controls.
- Prototype and test service changes with patients, carers, and staff.

## Band 7: Senior service designer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Senior service designer**

> A senior service designer is an experienced designer who works with minimal support and can influence and mentor others.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - work with service managers and programme directors to develop design concepts
> - potentially have responsibility across complex services
> - help set direction and embed good practice within teams
> - make important decisions based on research and understand how this research impacts others

### Responsibilities

- Lead the design of complex services that span several care settings or organizations.
- Shape the service vision with clinical, operational, and digital leaders.
- Design new service models that improve safety, access, and equity, and test them with real users.
- Make sure service changes are assessed for clinical risk, equality, and information governance effects.
- Mentor designers and embed service design practice in teams.

## Band 8b: Lead service designer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Lead service designer**

> A lead service designer is an expert practitioner who influences and mentors others.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - work with service managers and programme directors to develop design concepts
> - set direction and assure the quality of design delivery across teams
> - lead multiple or highly complex services

### Responsibilities

- Set the design direction for multiple services or a whole care pathway, and assure the quality of design across teams.
- Work with senior clinical and operational leaders to redesign how care is organized and delivered.
- Make sure designs work for people most affected by health inequalities and for staff under pressure.
- Set standards for how teams design for clinical safety and for assisted routes.
- Line manage and develop service designers.

## Band 8c: Head of service design

**UK GDaD PCF level: Head of service design**

> A head of service design is an expert practitioner with broad industry experience, who can define and assure best practice while influencing, leading and mentoring others.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - influence both design and organisational strategy and priorities
> - collaborate with counterpart colleagues across government
> - focus on ensuring the right conditions and environment for designers to work effectively

### Responsibilities

- Lead the service design profession and set its strategy, standards, and ways of working.
- Influence organizational strategy so that services are designed around the needs of patients, carers, and staff.
- Build service design capability, including recruitment, career paths, and training.
- Make the conditions for good design, such as access to users, time to research, and design in governance.
- Work with peers across the health and care system to design joined-up services.
