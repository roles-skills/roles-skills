## Summary

Incident managers restore normal service as quickly as possible when something goes wrong, and keep the effect on users as small as possible. They coordinate technical teams and suppliers, keep users and leaders informed, and make sure incidents are recorded and passed to problem management. In health care, incidents can directly affect patient care, so incident managers work closely with clinical and operational leaders.

## In a digital health care organization

- An outage of a clinical system can delay treatment, so incidents are prioritized by clinical impact, and clinical leads may need to start downtime procedures.
- Major incidents can happen at any time, so incident managers take part in on-call rotas and may be called out at night or at weekends.
- A cyber attack, such as ransomware, can become a major incident that affects the whole organization and lasts for days or weeks.
- Major IT incidents may need to link with the organization's emergency preparedness and business continuity arrangements.
- Incidents that may have harmed patients are also reported through the clinical incident process and reviewed with clinical safety.

## UK GDaD PCF role description (English original)

> Incident managers aim to restore normal service operation as quickly as possible and minimise any adverse effects on business operations. This ensures that the best possible levels of service quality and availability are returned and maintained.

## Band 5: Incident manager

**UK GDaD PCF level: Incident manager**

> An incident manager oversees incidents and restores normal operations as quickly as possible, with the least impact on the business or the user.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - work on-site, maintaining the hardware and fixing technical problems as quickly as possible
> - provide first-time resolution by troubleshooting and diagnosing or escalating faults (or both) to the major incident managers and problem managers to investigate and resolve
> - coach apprentices and share knowledge with team colleagues
> Your responsibilities may, in some departments, mirror those of an end user computing engineer.

### Responsibilities

- Coordinate the response to incidents affecting clinical and corporate services, from first report to restored service.
- Assess the clinical and operational impact of each incident and set its priority.
- Keep affected users, the service desk, and clinical leads informed with clear, timely updates.
- Escalate incidents to the major incident manager when they meet the agreed criteria.
- Make sure incidents are recorded accurately and passed to problem management.
- Take part in the on-call rota for out-of-hours incidents.

## Band 7: Major incident manager

**UK GDaD PCF level: Major incident manager**

> A major incident manager manages significant outages and crises and reports issues to problem management. At this level, you will work closely with workplace services, infrastructure, applications operators and communication managers. In some departments, you may also be the head of service.

### Responsibilities

- Lead the response to major incidents, such as outages of the electronic patient record, network failures, and cyber attacks.
- Run incident calls that bring together technical teams, suppliers, clinical leads, and communications staff.
- Advise clinical and operational leaders on when to start and stop downtime and business continuity procedures.
- Brief senior leaders and, where needed, link with the organization's emergency response arrangements.
- Lead post-incident reviews and make sure lessons are learned and acted on.
- Develop and test major incident plans with business continuity and emergency planning leads.

### Typical qualifications and experience

- Substantial experience of managing incidents in complex, high-availability services.
- An advanced IT service management qualification, or equivalent experience.

### Band outline

- **Knowledge:** Highly developed specialist knowledge, typically to master's level or equivalent experience.
- **Autonomy:** Works to organizational policy; decides how results are achieved; is the expert others consult.
- **Scope:** Several products or services, or a specialist function.
- **Leadership:** Leads a team or a professional practice area.
- **Accountability:** Delivery of a service or specialist function, and its budget if held.
