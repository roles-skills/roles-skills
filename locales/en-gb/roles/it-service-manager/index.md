# IT service manager

> This is an illustrative reference profile for a generic digital health care organisation. It is not an official job description for any employer, and its job evaluation scores are not a formal evaluation.

**Family:** [IT operations](../../#it-operations)  
**Bands:** 4, 6, 7, 8b  
**UK GDaD PCF role:** [IT service manager](https://understand-digital-data-roles-skills.service.gov.uk/role/it-service-manager/)  
**ESCO occupations:** [ICT operations manager](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/f0ca39a8-1e30-487c-95f9-ef2d5e734adc) (ISCO-08 1330)

## Summary

IT service managers make sure the organisation's digital services are delivered to agreed levels and keep improving. They define service levels, manage the service catalogue, report on performance, and coordinate the teams and suppliers that run each service. In health care, they turn what clinical services need, such as round-the-clock availability, into service targets and support arrangements.

## In a digital health care organisation

- Service levels for clinical systems reflect clinical risk, so targets for the electronic patient record or prescribing differ from those for a corporate system.
- Services are often provided by a mix of in-house teams, suppliers, and services shared with partner organisations, so service managers coordinate across organisational boundaries.
- Clinical business continuity depends on IT, so service managers work with clinical and emergency planning leads on downtime and continuity plans.
- Clinical staff have little time to report problems, so service managers use monitoring, service desk data, and clinical feedback to find issues early.

## Band 4: IT service analyst

**UK GDaD PCF level: IT service analyst**

> An IT service analyst is responsible for frontline technical support to end users on issues relating to hardware, software and peripherals. This will include responding to, documenting and resolving service desk incident tickets in a timely manner according to a service level agreement (SLA).
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - have excellent problem solving skills to diagnose, evaluate and resolve complex problem situations or, when appropriate, escalate or route them to IT resolver groups
> - perform root cause analysis
> - develop checklists for typical problems
> - recommend procedures and controls for problem prevention
> - work independently, share information and assist others with requests for service

### Responsibilities

- Respond to, document, and resolve incidents and requests within agreed service levels.
- Produce regular service reports, such as ticket volumes, response times, and the availability of clinical systems.
- Check that service catalogue entries and knowledge articles are accurate and up to date.
- Look for patterns in incidents and suggest improvements.
- Follow information governance rules when handling ticket and service data.

## Band 6: IT service manager

**UK GDaD PCF level: IT service manager**

> An IT service manager is responsible for defining service level agreements (SLAs) and operational level agreements (OLAs) across all relevant business functions.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - manage a fully functioning multi-sourced service support team with a customer-centric support approach across multiple locations, focused on service availability and performance
> - ensure that all service and support functions remain responsive to customer needs
> - ensure that the service catalogue and wider IT service operations knowledge library are maintained
> - ensure that adequate reporting and service standards are met for your specific service
> - ensure that all technical change is communicated and managed, with appropriate governance
> - ensure that a programme of continual service improvement is put in place across the wider service delivery function

### Responsibilities

- Define and agree service levels for clinical and corporate services, based on clinical risk and business need.
- Manage the support arrangements for a portfolio of services, including suppliers and shared services.
- Report service performance to service owners and clinical leads, and agree improvement actions.
- Make sure each service has tested business continuity and downtime arrangements.
- Keep the service catalogue current, and make sure staff are told about changes to their services.
- Run a programme of continual service improvement for your services.

## Band 7: Senior IT service manager

**UK GDaD PCF level: Senior IT service manager**

> A senior IT service manager is responsible for service management across platforms, multiple channels and services to ensure service availability, performance and process maturity.

### Responsibilities

- Lead service management across several platforms and channels, and improve the maturity of service management processes.
- Agree service levels and contract terms with major suppliers of clinical systems, and hold them to account.
- Present service performance and risks to senior clinical and operational leaders.
- Lead service reviews after major incidents, and track improvement actions to completion.
- Align IT continuity plans with clinical business continuity plans, working with emergency planning and clinical leads.
- Lead and develop IT service managers.

## Band 8b: Head of IT service management

**UK GDaD PCF level: Head of IT service management**

> A head of IT service management is responsible for setting the vision and strategy for service management, ensuring processes are owned and maturing.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - lead a team, ensuring adequate resources and capacity
> - represent the IT service management function at a senior level
> - act as an escalation point for business stakeholders

### Responsibilities

- Set the vision, strategy, and operating model for IT service management across the organisation.
- Make sure every service management process has an owner and keeps maturing.
- Represent IT service management at senior level, including at clinical and operational governance meetings.
- Act as the senior escalation point for clinical and corporate service leaders.
- Make sure the service management function has the people, tools, and budget it needs.
