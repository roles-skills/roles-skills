# Project support officer

> This is an illustrative reference profile for a generic digital health care organisation. It is not an official job description for any employer, and its job evaluation scores are not a formal evaluation.

**Family:** [Corporate services](../../#corporate-services)  
**Bands:** 3, 4, 5, 6  
**UK GDaD PCF role:** none (this reference defines the role)  
**ESCO occupations:** [project support officer](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/5ccb2c34-e860-43a5-9869-342487518115) (ISCO-08 3343)

## Summary

Project support officers help projects and programmes run smoothly by maintaining plans, registers, and documents, organising meetings, and producing reports. At senior levels they run the project management office (PMO), giving leaders a clear view of progress, risks, and benefits across the organisation's portfolio of digital change.

## In a digital health care organisation

- Digital health projects need clinical safety, information governance, and security approvals at set points, so project support tracks these as key milestones.
- Projects often depend on clinicians and care staff whose time is limited, so planning around clinical rotas and service pressures matters.
- Portfolio reporting helps leaders decide which changes go ahead when clinical services are under pressure.

## Band 3: Project support officer

Gives administrative support to one or more projects, maintaining records and organising meetings under the guidance of a project manager.

### Responsibilities

- Maintain project documents, with version control, in the project repository.
- Update action, risk, issue, and decision logs.
- Organise project meetings, send papers, and take action notes.
- Collect updates from the team for status reports.
- Handle routine project correspondence and enquiries.

### Typical qualifications and experience

- A level 3 qualification in business administration or project support, or equivalent experience.

### Band outline

- **Knowledge:** Knowledge of a range of procedures, some non-routine; typically a level 3 qualification or equivalent experience.
- **Autonomy:** Works within procedures; decides how to organise own work; refers problems upward.
- **Scope:** Own work and some tasks for the wider team.
- **Leadership:** May allocate routine work or train others in procedures.
- **Accountability:** Accuracy of own work and of records kept.

## Band 4: Project coordinator

Coordinates project controls for several projects or a programme, maintaining plans and registers and producing regular reports.

### Responsibilities

- Maintain project plans, registers, and trackers for several projects.
- Produce regular status and highlight reports.
- Track project budgets and spending, and raise variances.
- Check that projects follow the agreed method, including clinical safety and information governance steps.
- Coordinate project boards, papers, and actions.
- Support project managers with stakeholder communications.

## Band 5: PMO analyst

Analyses progress, risk, and benefits across a programme or portfolio, and runs project management office processes.

### Responsibilities

- Analyse progress, risks, issues, dependencies, and benefits across projects.
- Produce portfolio dashboards and reports for programme and portfolio boards.
- Run planning, change control, and assurance processes.
- Help project managers set up projects with the right controls and documents.
- Maintain the PMO's tools, templates, and guidance.
- Coach project support officers.

## Band 6: PMO manager

Manages the project management office for a programme or portfolio, setting standards and giving leaders reliable insight into delivery.

### Responsibilities

- Lead the project management office and its staff.
- Set project and portfolio standards, methods, and tools.
- Give senior leaders clear insight into portfolio progress, risks, and benefits.
- Run portfolio governance, including prioritisation, gateway reviews, and change control.
- Monitor programme budgets and resources, and report on them.
- Coach project managers on method and controls.
