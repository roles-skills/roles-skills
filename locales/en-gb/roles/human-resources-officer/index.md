# Human resources officer

> This is an illustrative reference profile for a generic digital health care organisation. It is not an official job description for any employer, and its job evaluation scores are not a formal evaluation.

**Family:** [Human resources](../../#human-resources)  
**Bands:** 3, 4, 5, 6  
**UK GDaD PCF role:** none (this reference defines the role)  
**ESCO occupations:** [human resources assistant](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/d8ed1703-9c03-4ea0-83cc-b6169bc54ffd) (ISCO-08 4416); [human resources officer](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/d3e32e5e-7f24-48e3-b939-e4f800eb62fb) (ISCO-08 2423)

## Summary

HR officers and advisers run the organisation's core people services, from contracts, starters, and changes to advice on people policies and support for managers with employee relations cases. They keep employment records accurate and confidential, help managers treat staff fairly and lawfully, and support the people who run digital and clinical services that never close.

## In a digital health care organisation

- Many staff work in services that run 24 hours a day, so HR teams advise on shift patterns, on-call and standby arrangements, rest breaks, and working time.
- Some cases are linked to patient safety incidents, safeguarding concerns, or professional registration, so HR staff work closely with clinical leads and follow the right processes for each.
- Digital, data, and technology staff are in short supply, so fair and quick HR processes, from contracts to flexible working requests, help the organisation keep the people it has.
- HR records are highly confidential personal data, and some include health information, so HR staff apply information governance rules every day.
- Staff in this reference are paid on a banded pay structure with job evaluation, so HR staff explain how bands, increments, and allowances work.

## Band 3: HR assistant

Provides accurate administrative support across the employment life cycle, working to set procedures and service standards with an HR officer or adviser close by.

### Responsibilities

- Prepare contracts, offer letters, and variation letters from standard templates.
- Enter starters, changes, and leavers in the HR and payroll system before payroll deadlines.
- Answer routine questions from staff and managers by phone, email, and the HR helpdesk.
- Take notes at formal meetings, such as attendance and grievance meetings, and keep case files in order.
- Keep personal files and records confidential, accurate, and in line with retention rules.

### Typical qualifications and experience

- A level 2 or 3 qualification, a business administration apprenticeship, or equivalent experience.
- Experience of accurate administrative work with confidential information.

### Band outline

- **Knowledge:** Knowledge of a range of procedures, some non-routine; typically a level 3 qualification or equivalent experience.
- **Autonomy:** Works within procedures; decides how to organise own work; refers problems upward.
- **Scope:** Own work and some tasks for the wider team.
- **Leadership:** May allocate routine work or train others in procedures.
- **Accountability:** Accuracy of own work and of records kept.

## Band 4: HR officer

Runs HR administration for a group of services and gives first-line advice to managers on people policies, checking the work of HR assistants.

### Responsibilities

- Run starters, changes, and leavers for a group of services, solving unusual or complex cases.
- Advise managers on routine policy questions, such as annual leave, flexible working, and attendance triggers.
- Check the work of HR assistants and train them on processes and the HR system.
- Produce standard workforce reports, such as absence and turnover, for managers.
- Support HR advisers with employee relations cases by preparing papers and arranging hearings.
- Suggest improvements to HR processes, templates, and self-service guidance.

## Band 5: HR adviser

Gives professional HR advice to managers in a group of services and manages a caseload of employee relations cases, working to defined policies with a senior HR adviser available for complex cases.

### Responsibilities

- Advise managers on conduct, capability, attendance, grievance, and dignity at work cases, from first concern to outcome.
- Support formal hearings, advising the panel on policy, process, and fair outcomes.
- Advise on reasonable adjustments, flexible working, and occupational health referrals.
- Advise managers on working time, on-call, and rota rules for services that run 24 hours a day.
- Support small organisational changes, such as changes to working patterns, including consultation with staff.
- Train managers in people policies and how to apply them.

## Band 6: Senior HR adviser

Leads HR advice for a directorate, handles the most complex and sensitive cases, and supports HR advisers, working with broad freedom within the organisation's people policies.

### Responsibilities

- Manage complex and high-risk employee relations cases, including those linked to patient safety incidents or safeguarding.
- Lead or commission investigations and coach managers who investigate.
- Lead organisational change for a service, such as a restructure or a new on-call rota, including formal consultation.
- Advise on banding for new and changed roles and take part in job evaluation panels.
- Analyse case and workforce data for the directorate and recommend action on trends.
- Draft and review people policies, working with staff representatives.
- Supervise and develop HR advisers.

- A recognised HR professional qualification at intermediate or advanced level, or equivalent experience.
- Substantial experience of managing complex employee relations cases and organisational change.

- **Knowledge:** Specialist knowledge across a range of procedures, built through further training or experience.

- **Scope:** A product, service, or workstream.
- **Leadership:** May lead a small team or mentor colleagues.
- **Accountability:** Outcomes of own workstream and quality of advice given.
