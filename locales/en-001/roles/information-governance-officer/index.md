# Information governance officer

> This is an illustrative reference profile for a generic digital health care organisation. It is not an official job description for any employer, and its job evaluation scores are not a formal evaluation.

**Family:** [Information governance](../../#information-governance)  
**Bands:** 4, 5, 6, 7, 8a  
**UK GDaD PCF role:** none (this reference defines the role)  
**ESCO occupations:** [data protection officer](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/1619d7f3-7d95-407c-a20e-47745bfcde73) (ISCO-08 2619); [document management officer](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/ef5b17a5-3ca8-4965-bd3d-61c4aca490d1) (ISCO-08 1349)

## Summary

Information governance staff help the organisation handle personal and health information lawfully, securely, and ethically. They advise teams on data protection and confidentiality, handle information rights requests, support data protection impact assessments, manage data breaches, and gather the evidence that shows the organisation meets information governance standards.

## In a digital health care organisation

- Data protection law gives health information extra protection, so the bar for lawful use, security, and transparency is high.
- Digital health services often share data across many care organisations, so information sharing agreements and data flows need careful review.
- Patients and the public have legal rights to access their records and the organisation's information, with strict time limits.
- Data breaches involving health information can cause real harm and distress, so incidents need fast, careful handling.
- New uses of data, such as analytics, research, and AI, raise questions of consent, confidentiality, and public trust.

## Band 4: Information governance officer

Supports the information governance team with information rights requests, incident logging, training records, and evidence gathering, following set procedures.

### Responsibilities

- Log, acknowledge, and track information rights requests, and gather information for responses.
- Log data breaches and near misses, and gather the facts for review.
- Keep information governance registers up to date, such as the information asset register and data flow map.
- Collect evidence for the annual information governance self-assessment.
- Track staff completion of information governance training.
- Answer routine information governance questions from staff.

## Band 5: Senior information governance officer

Handles a caseload of information rights requests and incidents, and supports teams with data protection impact assessments and information sharing.

### Responsibilities

- Handle information rights requests, including redaction and applying exemptions, within legal time limits.
- Assess and investigate data breaches and near misses, and track actions to close.
- Help project teams complete data protection impact assessments and describe data flows.
- Draft information sharing agreements and privacy notices for review.
- Deliver information governance training and briefings to staff.
- Carry out access and record-keeping audits.

## Band 6: Information governance lead

Gives expert information governance advice to a directorate or programme, leading data protection impact assessments and complex cases.

### Responsibilities

- Advise product teams and services on lawful basis, consent, confidentiality, and retention.
- Review data protection impact assessments for complex systems and recommend controls.
- Handle complex or contentious information rights requests and internal reviews.
- Investigate serious data breaches and recommend whether to notify regulators.
- Review information sharing agreements with partner organisations.
- Supervise and coach information governance officers.

## Band 7: Information governance manager

Manages the information governance service day to day, including its team, casework, assurance programme, and training.

### Responsibilities

- Manage the information governance team and its casework, making sure legal time limits are met.
- Plan and run the information governance assurance programme and annual self-assessment.
- Write and review information governance policies and procedures.
- Advise senior managers on information risks and keep the information risk register.
- Lead the response to serious incidents, working with security, clinical safety, and communications colleagues.
- Design and oversee information governance training for all staff.

## Band 8a: Head of information governance

Leads information governance for the organisation, setting its policy and strategy and advising senior leaders on information risk and compliance.

### Responsibilities

- Set the information governance strategy, policies, and framework for the organisation.
- Lead the information governance and records services and develop their staff.
- Advise the executive lead for information risk, the board, and its committees on information risk and compliance.
- Make the final decisions on complex disclosure, sharing, and breach notification questions.
- Represent the organisation with regulators, auditors, and partner organisations.
- Make sure new digital services, data uses, and AI are designed with privacy and transparency from the start.
