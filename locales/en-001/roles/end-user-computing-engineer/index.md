# End user computing engineer

> This is an illustrative reference profile for a generic digital health care organisation. It is not an official job description for any employer, and its job evaluation scores are not a formal evaluation.

**Family:** [IT operations](../../#it-operations)  
**Bands:** 4, 5, 6, 7, 8b  
**UK GDaD PCF role:** [End user computing engineer](https://understand-digital-data-roles-skills.service.gov.uk/role/end-user-computing-engineer/)  
**ESCO occupations:** [ICT technician](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/3e7bf729-4442-4b9f-ad5e-83111963795c) (ISCO-08 3512)

## Summary

End user computing engineers provide, support, and maintain the devices and software that staff use every day, such as laptops, desktops, tablets, mobile phones, printers, and the shared workstations on wards and in clinics. They fix faults on site and remotely, roll out and update devices, and keep them secure and compliant. Their work keeps clinicians and care staff able to see and record patient information at the point of care.

## In a digital health care organisation

- Many devices are shared by clinical staff on wards, in clinics, and in emergency departments, often with fast user switching and smartcard or tap-in login.
- A broken device in a clinical area can delay care, so engineers prioritise those faults and keep spare devices ready to swap.
- Engineers work in clinical areas, so they follow infection prevention and control rules, respect patient privacy and dignity, and fit their work around clinical activity.
- Devices hold or display confidential patient information, so they must be encrypted, patched, managed, and securely wiped or destroyed at the end of their life.
- Some peripherals support clinical processes or connect to clinical equipment, such as wristband and label printers, barcode scanners, and dictation devices, so changes to them need care and testing.

## UK GDaD PCF role description (English original)

> An end user computing engineer is responsible for managing the product life cycle of all service-raised incidents (incident control) and all service requests (request control), requiring the use of knowledge management. In this role, you will also be responsible for informing customers on progress and advising on workarounds where necessary, as well as the support and maintenance of end user services.

## Band 4: Associate end user computing engineer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Associate end user computing engineer**

> As a trainee, you will work in an established team and support end user computing engineers in their daily work, under their direction.

### Responsibilities

- Set up, replace, and repair devices and peripherals under the direction of experienced engineers.
- Fix common faults in clinical and office areas, such as printer, scanner, and login problems.
- Follow infection prevention and control rules, and respect patient privacy, when working on wards and in clinics.
- Record work, assets, and parts accurately in the service management tool.
- Work towards an IT support apprenticeship or equivalent qualification.

## Band 5: End user computing engineer

**UK GDaD PCF level: End user computing engineer**

> An end user computing engineer works on-site, maintaining hardware and fixing technical problems as quickly as possible.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - provide first-time resolution by troubleshooting and diagnosing or escalating faults (or both) to service desk managers, incident managers and problem managers to investigate and resolve
> - coach associates and share knowledge with team colleagues

### Responsibilities

- Diagnose and fix hardware and software faults on site and remotely, aiming to resolve them at the first visit.
- Respond quickly to faults in clinical areas, and provide a replacement device when a repair would delay care.
- Build, deploy, and update devices using the organisation's standard images and device management tools.
- Keep devices patched, encrypted, and compliant, and securely wipe or dispose of them at the end of their life.
- Configure and test clinical peripherals, such as wristband printers, label printers, and barcode scanners.
- Coach associate engineers and share fixes through knowledge articles.

## Band 6: Senior end user computing engineer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Senior end user computing engineer**

> A senior end user computing engineer is responsible for managing the team but also gets involved in solving technical problems.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - manage, co-ordinate and prioritise tasks to resolve technical incidents as quickly as possible
> - work closely with infrastructure, capacity managers and availability managers to fix technical problems and identify and escalate repeat issues to the lead end user computing engineer, the incident or problem manager and software developers

### Responsibilities

- Lead a team of engineers day to day, prioritising work by its effect on patient care and staff.
- Plan and run device rollouts and refreshes for wards, clinics, and community teams, with as little disruption to care as possible.
- Test device builds and updates with clinical applications and peripherals before they are rolled out.
- Make sure devices meet security standards, including patch levels, encryption, and endpoint protection.
- Support go-lives of clinical systems with floor walking and rapid device fixes.
- Work with infrastructure, application, and security teams to fix complex and repeated faults.

## Band 8b: Principal end user computing engineer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Principal end user computing engineer**

> A principal end user computing engineer owns the strategy roadmap and leads on resourcing, learning and development for their team. At this level, you look at the bigger picture and understand trends in the business.

### Responsibilities

- Own the end user computing strategy and roadmap, including how staff will reach clinical systems in hospitals, clinics, patients' homes, and the community.
- Make sure the device estate supports safe, fast access to patient information at the point of care.
- Advise senior leaders on device investment, risks, and the costs of refreshes and new technologies.
- Lead the response to estate-wide device risks, such as operating systems reaching end of support and resilience against ransomware.
- Lead resourcing, learning, and development for end user computing teams.
- Work with health and care partners on shared device and access standards.

### Typical qualifications and experience

- Extensive experience of leading end user computing for a large, multi-site organisation.

### Band outline

- **Knowledge:** Expert knowledge across several disciplines or a large service.
- **Autonomy:** Shapes policy and strategy for a large area.
- **Scope:** Several services or teams, or a principal-level discipline.
- **Leadership:** Manages managers, or is the principal authority in a discipline.
- **Accountability:** Several services, their staff, and their budgets.
