# Frontend developer

> This is an illustrative reference profile for a generic digital health care organisation. It is not an official job description for any employer, and its job evaluation scores are not a formal evaluation.

**Family:** [Software development](../../#software-development)  
**Bands:** 4, 5, 6, 7, 8b, 8c  
**UK GDaD PCF role:** [Frontend developer](https://understand-digital-data-roles-skills.service.gov.uk/role/frontend-developer/)  
**ESCO occupations:** [web developer](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/c40a2919-48a9-40ea-b506-1f34f693496d) (ISCO-08 2513); [user interface developer](http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/866c7813-2c03-47d7-9bdc-192cfbace57c) (ISCO-08 2512)

## Summary

Frontend developers build the user interfaces of the organisation's digital health services, such as patient-facing websites and apps, online forms, and screens that clinicians and care staff use during care. They write accessible, fast, and secure code that works for everyone, including people who are unwell, anxious, or using older devices, and they work in multidisciplinary product teams with designers, researchers, and clinicians.

## In a digital health care organisation

- Many users are unwell, in pain, older, or disabled, so accessibility and plain, calm interfaces are essential rather than optional.
- A confusing or wrong display of clinical information, such as a medication dose, a result, or an appointment time, can harm patients, so frontend work follows the clinical risk management process.
- Interfaces often show confidential health information, so developers design for privacy, for example on shared devices and in busy clinical areas.
- Clinicians use interfaces under time pressure and with frequent interruptions, so screens must be quick, predictable, and hard to misread.
- Patients reach services on a wide range of devices and connections, so services use progressive enhancement and work on slow networks.

## UK GDaD PCF role description (English original)

> A frontend developer designs, builds and improves website software that meets user needs.
> 
> In this role, you will:
> - be responsible for writing clean, accessible code following a progressive enhancement approach
> - create code that is open by default and easy for others to reuse
> - design software which meets user needs and creates meaningful interactions and relationships with users
> - have an understanding of the three fundamental frontend technologies (HTML, CSS and JavaScript)

## Band 4: Apprentice frontend developer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Apprentice frontend developer**

> An apprentice frontend developer attends certified training and develops skills on the job.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - spend a substantial portion of time shadowing others
> - work with other developers to write code and tests
> - write code that follows web standards
> - build your understanding of accessibility
> - build your knowledge
> - develop software which meets user needs

### Responsibilities

- Learn the team's design system, languages, and tools through structured training and pairing.
- Build small interface components under close supervision, following the organisation's design system.
- Test components with assistive technologies, such as screen readers, with support from colleagues.
- Follow information governance and clinical safety procedures when working with test and live data.
- Work towards an apprenticeship or equivalent qualification.

- Build and test interface features for patient-facing and staff-facing services, working from user stories with guidance.

## Band 6: Frontend developer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Frontend developer**

> A frontend developer builds software components, primarily in the form of user interfaces.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - collaborate and work closely with other disciplines to develop designs and working prototypes based on user needs and in support of organisational aims
> - develop software components that form part of a product to meet user needs
> - write clean, well-tested code that follows web standards
> - coach and mentor more junior colleagues

### Responsibilities

- Design and build accessible, responsive interfaces for health services independently, choosing suitable approaches.
- Work with designers and user researchers to test prototypes with patients, clinicians, and care staff.
- Build interfaces that call health data APIs, such as HL7 FHIR services, and handle errors and missing data safely.
- Take part in hazard workshops and build safety controls into the interface, such as confirmation steps and clear warnings.
- Protect confidential information in the browser, for example by managing sessions and caching carefully on shared devices.
- Review code and mentor junior and apprentice frontend developers.

## Band 7: Senior frontend developer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Senior frontend developer**

> A senior frontend developer is an experienced developer who works with minimal support and can influence and mentor others.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - help set direction and embed good practice within teams
> - make decisions based on research
> - plan and lead development on sets of related stories, working with other disciplines to understand what needs to be built
> - have an understanding of the whole system
> - teach and mentor others

### Responsibilities

- Lead the frontend design and delivery of significant features, such as booking, results, or clinical workflow screens.
- Set and maintain frontend standards for the team, including accessibility, performance, security, and testing.
- Contribute components and patterns to the organisation's design system and make sure they are safe and accessible.
- Work with clinical safety officers to make sure interface hazards are identified, controlled, and tested.
- Make sure services work well on slow connections, older devices, and assistive technologies.
- Coach and develop frontend developers in the team.

## Band 8b: Lead frontend developer

**UK GDaD PCF level: Lead frontend developer**

> A lead frontend developer is an expert practitioner who influences and mentors others.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - set direction and assure the quality of development across teams
> - lead multiple or highly complex services
> - contribute to the community, providing technical leadership, coaching and mentoring
> - promote knowledge sharing and adoption of good practice
> - be proficient in a wide range of technical systems and able to identify appropriate technology and approaches

### Responsibilities

- Lead frontend engineering across a group of teams or a major patient-facing service.
- Own the technical direction of the organisation's design system and frontend libraries.
- Make sure interfaces across services meet accessibility standards and are assured for clinical safety.
- Work with architects, designers, and clinical leads to shape product roadmaps and frontend technology choices.
- Lead the frontend community of practice and line manage or lead frontend developers.

## Band 8c: Head of frontend development

**UK GDaD PCF level: Head of frontend development**

> A head of frontend development is an expert practitioner with broad industry experience. They are an expert in many technical areas, or specialists with very deep knowledge in a particular technical area.
> 
> At this role level, you will:
> - define and assure best practice while influencing, leading and mentoring others
> - influence and develop organisational strategy and priorities, collaborating with counterpart colleagues across government
> - ensure the right conditions and environment for frontend developers to work effectively
> - develop departmental capability by facilitating internal mobility, shaping career paths and recruiting talent

### Responsibilities

- Set the frontend strategy, standards, and practices for all of the organisation's digital services.
- Be accountable for the accessibility and usability of the organisation's interfaces for patients and staff.
- Advise senior leaders on frontend technology, design system investment, and the risks of poor interfaces to care.
- Represent the organisation in cross-organisation design system and accessibility communities.
- Lead and develop frontend developers across the organisation, including recruitment and career paths.
