# Performance review: a guide for managers

Use the reference profiles to give your team members a clear, shared statement of what their role expects at their band. Then use that statement in appraisals and development conversations.

## Principles

- **Development first.** The profiles help people see where they are and where they could grow. They are not a scoring system for pay or discipline.
- **The job description comes first.** A reference profile is illustrative. Where it differs from someone's actual job description, objectives, or evaluated job, those take priority.
- **Evidence, not impressions.** Agree ratings from examples of work, not from general impressions.
- **Expect a profile, not perfection.** Most people meet most expectations, exceed some, and are developing in a few.

## Before the review

1. Agree with the team member which role and level they will use. Sometimes this is a mix of two roles.
2. Ask them to complete a [self assessment](../self-assessment/) and share it a few days before the review.
3. Read their ratings and evidence. Note any ratings where your view differs, with examples.

## In the review

1. **Start with strengths.** Look at skills rated at or above the expected level, and the evidence for them.
2. **Agree ratings.** Where your view differs, discuss the evidence. Record your view in **manager_rating**, and agree a final rating where you can.
3. **Agree two or three priorities.** Choose the gaps that matter most for the role and the team over the next six to twelve months.
4. **Agree actions.** For each priority, write a specific, achievable **development_action**, and say what support you will give.
5. **Talk about progression.** If they are interested, compare their profile with the next level up. The role page shows the band outline, responsibilities, skills, and job evaluation factors for each level, so the differences are concrete.

## After the review

- Keep the completed file with the appraisal record.
- Check progress on the actions at one-to-ones.
- Repeat the self assessment at the next review, so you can both see the change over time.

## Using job evaluation scores

Each role level shows illustrative scores on 16 job evaluation factors. They explain why the level sits in its band, for example more freedom to act, wider responsibility for people, or more complex judgements. Use them to talk about how jobs differ between bands.

Do not use them to re-band a job. A real evaluation scores the actual job, from an agreed job description, by a trained panel.

## Fairness

- Use the same role level and skills for people doing the same job.
- Watch for bias in ratings, for example rating confident people higher, or rating part-time staff lower.
- Make reasonable adjustments in how people take part, and consider how they show evidence.
- Agree every rating and action, rather than imposing them.
